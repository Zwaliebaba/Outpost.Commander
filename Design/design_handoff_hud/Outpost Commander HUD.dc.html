OUTPOST COMMANDER · MVP · IN-MATCH HUD · REV B
The tactical HUD, authored at 1440 × 960
Every frame is exactly 1440 × 960 authored pixels at 1:1, scaled 2× onto the 2880 × 1920 panel. All coordinates are integers. Rev B draws the scene through the real camera — 40° vertical FOV, 34° above the plane, focus distance 2,600 units — so ships scale and foreshorten with depth, world-space circles are ellipses, and the HUD sits over a perspective field rather than a flat one. Nothing in the interface pass changed dimension: it is still axis-aligned rects, line segments, flat triangles and Segoe UI glyph quads.
CHROME
Gunmetal scrim to the content edge, one hairline on the playfield-facing edge, registration ticks at its ends. Instrument, not glass. A panel with nothing to say is not drawn.
OWN vs HOSTILE
The cool band, 150–240°, is reserved for the local player forever. The three hostile hues sit at 78°, 268° and 318°. No team is red.
DEPTH
No horizon — the 30° pitch floor guarantees it never enters frame. Depth is read from ship scale, formation stretch and the shape of world circles.
STATE, NOT COLOUR ALONE
Every button state carries a geometric cue as well as a hue: an index bar, a bottom rule, a hatch, a top bar. Readable in peripheral vision and colour-blind safe.

SCREEN SPACE vs WORLD SPACE — THE TWO CIRCLES
{{ studyA }}
The double-tap circle is screen space: r = 192 authored pixels, a 64-segment line strip, a true circle on the glass at every camera angle, centred on the ship rather than the finger. It is the rule made visible. Because it is screen space, the camera is the group-size control — zoom in for a squad, out for the fleet.
{{ studyB }}
The placement radius is world space: 400 units on the plane, projected through the same transform, so it is an ellipse — wider and lower at the near side, compressed at the far. 48 segments, 24 drawn, dashed so it can never be mistaken for the selection circle. Getting this wrong is the classic tell that a 3D game's overlays were authored in 2D.
THE TWO BARS, AND HULL BARS IN THE WORLD
12
MINER
Four discriminators, not one. Hull is a single keylined trough with quarter index ticks, unbroken, cool white losing to red, always the upper row. Cargo is four separated chips, no trough, no ticks, warm gold, always the lower row. The cargo bar cannot display a value it does not have.
{{ studyC }}
World hull bars are billboarded and depth-invariant: 18 × 6 authored pixels whether the ship is 26 px across in the near rank or 8 px in the far one. Only the lost portion is bright; the remainder is near-black, and a ship at full hull draws nothing at all. A healthy fleet is a row of dark ticks, a dying one a row of red — loudness tracks severity for free, and it does not shrink into illegibility with distance.

ORDER MARKER — PROJECTED ONTO THE PLANE
{{ studyD }}
Drawn the instant the gesture resolves and 152 ms before any ship moves — the single most latency-critical element on screen. Rev B makes it a world object rather than a screen sprite: a 120-unit square footprint projected onto the plane (four line segments, so it foreshortens with everything else), plus a 90-unit vertical riser and a short cross-tick at its head so the marker is findable when the footprint is small. The group line meets the riser's base. All of it clears when the host acknowledges the sequence.

F · RATIONALE — NON-OBVIOUS CHOICES ONLY
Drawing the scene in perspective changed three overlays and no HUD numbers
The camera is 40° vertical FOV, 34° above the plane, focus at 2,600 units — inside the 30° pitch floor, so the horizon is never in frame and there is no sky-vs-ground line to lean on. Depth therefore has to come from the content: ship scale runs about 2.5:1 top to bottom of the frame, a formation's rank spacing stretches with it, and the fleet foreshortens on the plane. Three overlays had to change and they are the ones a 2D mock gets wrong: the placement radius is now an ellipse (world circle, raked camera), the order marker is a projected footprint with a riser rather than a screen-aligned square, and order lines run in perspective from each group centroid. Two things deliberately did not change: the selection circle stays a true screen-space circle, because that is exactly what makes the camera the group-size control; and world hull bars stay billboarded at a fixed 18 × 6, because a hull bar that shrinks with distance is unreadable precisely when the ship is too small to read.
The plane grid is an addition to the scene spec and needs a decision
§7 describes space as near-black with silhouettes, a star field and a galaxy band. It does not mention a grid, and these frames draw one: a 512-unit tactical lattice on the plane, every fourth line brighter, fading with depth, peak luminance about 7% — under ADR-019's 12% area ceiling. It is here because without a horizon it is the only cue that says this is a surface you are looking across, and it doubles as a distance scale for placing modules and reading fleet spacing. The cost is honest and small: about 56 line segments in one draw, no new texture, no new pass. It is also the one thing here that can be deleted without moving a single interface number — so if it is not wanted, cut it and nothing else changes.
Chrome: gunmetal instrument, not glass — and state is never colour alone
A hairline alone fails the worst case: over the galaxy band, 20 px Semibold on bare sky collapses wherever a bright silhouette passes under a glyph, and silhouettes move. The scrim at 0.88 over the band composites to roughly 3.5% luminance and holds about 10:1 unconditionally. What keeps it from eating the battlefield is extent, not transparency: it stops at the content box, every panel is edge-anchored, and a panel with no content is not drawn — Frame 1 covers 3.5% of the frame. Rev B pulled the chrome off blue toward gunmetal (#313A40 / #5A6A72) so the only cool saturated thing on screen is your own fleet, and added the instrument register the brief's device implies: registration ticks at the ends of each panel rule and at the frame's left and right quarter points, a coloured index bar down the leading edge of every target, and quarter index ticks in the hull trough. Each of those is one or two rects. Every state now has a geometric cue as well as a hue — index bar, bottom rule, hatch, top bar — so the build panel survives both peripheral vision and colour blindness.
Own vs hostile: reserve a hue band, not a colour
Team colour is a vertex attribute, so at a 4 px silhouette hue is the only channel — no outline, no badge, no shape difference. Four pretty colours would solve the MVP and break at M4. Instead the cool band 150–240° belongs to the local player permanently and every hostile is drawn outside it: 78°, 268°, 318°. "Is that mine" becomes a warm/cool judgement, which is pre-attentive and survives the band. Telling hostile A from hostile B is a slower, rarer read and gets the leftovers, exactly as the brief ranks it. The red trap is closed by excluding 340–45° from the team set entirely: one plausible team colour given up, an alert channel bought that can never be mistaken for a fleet.
Alert: position carries the bearing, one triangle carries the rest
A rectangle cannot point. Direction is therefore carried twice: coarsely by which edge and where along it the indicator sits (the crossing of the centre→event ray with the frame, which the new quarter-point registration ticks give a scale to), and locally by one outward triangle. The 8 px stripe flush to the edge does the noticing — a hard bright line against near-black in peripheral vision, pulsing on a 1,200 ms linear triangle, which the eye catches without foveating. The count is the only thing to read and it is a single glanceable number, so nothing invites a stop. Deliberately no scrim and no plate: the failure ADR-020 names is habituation, and habituation starts the moment an alert looks like furniture.
Quit: two taps, and the arming target stays at the floor
An accidental hit is not acceptable. There is no pause, no save and no rejoin-in-progress, so one stray contact at the top of the frame ends a five-minute match with no recovery path anywhere in the system. So: tap one arms, tap two quits, and the confirm expires after 4,000 ms so an armed state cannot be left lying on the glass. The arming target stays at the 48 px floor rather than a comfort tier — it is the one control on screen that should be slightly hard to hit — and the confirm states the stake rather than asking a polite question: NO SAVE. NO REJOIN. STAY is the lit, left-hand choice; QUIT is outlined only, and is the only red target in the design that is not reporting damage.
Deviations, and one hole the brief did not name
Diagonals appear in exactly two places and both earn it. The alert triangle, because direction needs one; and the 45° hatch on an unavailable button — about 14 line segments, 12 px apart — because the two dead states must differ by something other than a value, and a texture difference reads at a glance where a grey-vs-grey does not. Everything else in the interface pass is axis-aligned.

One line per design group, not per ship. §6 says "lines"; thirty converging lines is noise and thirty quads. Four maximum.

The 32 px size is on four numbers, not only the build buttons. "SHIPYARD L1" cannot be set at 32 px inside any button that also fits six across the frame. The large size lands on the cost figure — the thing the unaffordable state modifies and the thing a player compares — and on the three other numbers read mid-fight. Names are 20 px.

Clear and cancel are 96 × 64 with words, not 64 × 64 with glyphs. An × is two diagonals and aliases badly with no multisampling; the words cost 32 px of panel width and are unambiguous in a design with no tooltips.

The queue slot conflict is unresolved and is not an interface problem. One slot, six always-live buttons, credits spent at start: tapping a button while something is building replaces it and the spent credits are gone. Dimming the six while the slot is busy would overload "unavailable" with a second meaning. A refund-on-replace rule in GameCore is the fix, and it is a simulation decision.

Elapsed match time is available and is not shown. The tick gives it, but §3 fixes five surfaces and it belongs to none.

A standing selection has no world-side mark. Once the order marker clears, the panel is the only readout of what is selected — the stated design, and correct while a hand is on the glass. If M1 finds it wrong in the quiet moments, the cheapest fix inside the primitives is a 1 px team-colour underline beneath each selected ship: 110 quads worst case, no new colour, no new state.
What the brief asked for and the data cannot give
The credit readout carries a change flash and not a rate: a 120 × 3 rule beneath the number, cyan on a gain and amber on a spend, decaying exponentially. It answers "did that land" without implying a series the snapshot does not contain. No income rate, no ore remaining, no kill count, no enemy credits appear anywhere — each was wanted somewhere in this layout and each has no data path. The one that hurts is ore remaining in a field: a miner's standing order outlives the rock, and with no readout the player discovers it by watching a miner idle. That is a wire decision, not a HUD one.
