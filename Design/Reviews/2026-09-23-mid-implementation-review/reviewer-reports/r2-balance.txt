UNEDITED SPECIALIST REPORT, Phase 1 of the 2026-09-23 mid-implementation review. Written in isolation from the Lead's map; the synthesis in ../../2026-09-23-mid-implementation-review.md supersedes it where they disagree. File and line references are to commit 67a4ad8; paths under /tmp refer to the review session's scratch directory and are reproduced under ../harness/.

(a) Summary. I read GameDesign §3–§7/§10, OpenQuestions Q10/Q14/Q16/Q26/Q35/Q46/Q47/Q51/Q54/Q56, Plan M3.0/M3.5/M3.6/M3.9/M3.11 and M4.4/M4.7, Interface §6, and GameCore Catalog/Design/DerivedStats/Generator/ModuleSite/Layout plus GameLogic MiningSystem/Economy/BuildSystem/ModuleEffects/UnloadTarget/Tick/World, then extended the Lead's harness (scratchpad/r2/sim.cpp, compiled with g++ -O2 against the unmodified simulation; output in scratchpad/r2/results-final.txt) to drive fifteen scripted build orders through the real BuildSystem with modules placed and upgraded, a per-rock finite-ore model with M3.9-style nearest-with-ore retargeting layered over the unmodified MiningSystem, the home field radially remapped, a forward unload point, the four-player field, and §7's raid table under three cadences (all combat figures below assume continuous DPS, i.e. M3.0's hundredths option; 1 Hz integer-per-shot changes them by ≤4%; per-tick integer truncation gives zero MassDriver damage). Key results: with the shipped field four miners already exceed the 20 cr/s slot and ShipyardL2 lines field 28 fighters at 300 s against 18 without while the ore processor never adds one; under Q26's 200 ore the home field is dry at 106–141 s and the station idles 85–188 s of the match; with the home field at 1,200–2,000 units one miner earns 3.10 cr/s (six = 18.6, under the slot) and five openings land within 25%; a forward unload point at a contested cluster yields 6.7–9 cr/s per miner against 0.67–1.33; and a fighter at 401 units from the station reaches every point of the 160-unit unloading area while outside the 400-unit point defense.

(b) Findings, most severe first.

[R2-1] [Reviewer 2 Balance] [Blocker] [Coherence]
Claim: The station's safe zone is geometrically empty — a fighter at 401 units from the station is outside point defense (400) and inside MassDriver range (600) of every point of the 160-unit unloading area — so §5's "protected unloading area", §7's "about 3½ of six miners lost fleeing" and M3.6's miner flight protect nothing, and M3.11's questions 1 and 2 test a mechanic that does not exist.
Evidence: FACT — GameCore/Catalog.cpp:71 (MassDriver rangeUnits 600), :76 (PointDefense 400); GameLogic/UnloadTarget.cpp:23-28 and UnloadTarget.h:34 (unload reach (60+220)/2 + 20 = 160 for a Scout at a Station); the one range check that exists is centre-to-centre (MiningSystem.cpp:100); GameDesign §5 ("a raider that chases a fleeing miner home crosses 400 and dies in under six seconds"; "what the point defense protects is the unloading area"; "which side of your station you build on is a decision"); §7 flight row "about 3½"; Plan/M3-the-fight.md:183-204 (M3.5 done-when: "a fighter at 500 units takes no return fire") and :206-223 (M3.6 done-when: "§7's figure for miners lost fleeing 1,500 units is reproduced"); Q16 ("a real choice between escorting and expanding").
Scenario / proof: A miner unloading is within 160 of the station centre S. A fighter at 401 from S is at most 401 + 160 = 561 from any such miner; 561 < 600, so it fires, and 401 > 400, so the station never fires. From the 401 ring a fighter reaches the whole disc of radius 199 around S; the unload disc is 160. Flight: from the nearest shipped rock (797 units, harness §INCOME) a miner runs 637 units in 6.4 s; three fighters at 105 dps kill 1.5 miners en route (§7's own method) and the remaining 4.5 at the station from 401 — 6 of 6, not 3½. Pursuit inside 400 (the "loiterer" the PD kills) is never required; that M3.2's unwritten pursuit would stop at weapon range is INFERENCE, the geometry is FACT. Modules: from 401 a fighter reaches every module within 199 of S on any side and every near-side module; the far side is 9 s away (half the 401 ring = 1,260 units at 140 u/s), so "which side you build on" is a nine-second decision.
Why it matters: P1's "an economy you have to defend" needs somewhere to defend it to; M3.11 Q2 ("is the safe zone too safe?") is unanswerable as posed, Q1's "exchange rather than slaughter" will read as slaughter, and M3.6's exit criterion pins a false number. The design's two intents — a PD that protects unloading and a fighter that stands off at 500 unpunished — contradict each other by 200 units and one must be chosen.
Recommendation: PointDefense rangeUnits 400 → 480 (Catalog.cpp:76; ModuleSiteTests' "ring equals PD range" assert becomes ring 400 ≤ PD 480, ModuleSite.h:26-28), and M3.6's flight destination becomes the point 160 beyond the station on the ray away from the attacker (S + 160·unit(S − attacker)) instead of S: a fighter at 481 reaches the near side (321) but not the far side (641 > 600); the station stays shellable from 481–600 (a 119-unit band, one six-slot ring at 90 spacing); far-side modules (≥ 155 clearance) are safe from the ring and near-side ones are not, so §5's side sentence becomes true; "crosses 400 and dies" becomes "crosses 440". It needs R2-3's inner radius (at the shipped 600 a miner extracting at 400 from S would sit inside 480). §7's flight row from a 1,200–2,000 field: 2.5–4.4 of six die en route and the rest live only on the far side. Alternative: PointDefense range 800 with its Medium modifier 90 → 30 (2 × 60 × 0.3 = 36 dps; a fighter lasts 16.7 s inside 800): an unescorted siege needs nine fighters (2,700 cr) and loses six (208 HP per fighter-lifetime; 208 × (9+8+7+6+5+4) = 8,112 ≥ 8,000), a module raid needs four and loses two or three (833 + 625 + 417 = 1,875 ≥ 1,500), a field at 1,200+ stays raidable (≥ 1,000 from S while extracting) — at the cost of §5's near-side standoff, which disappears.
Confidence: High on the geometry (three catalog constants, one derivation); Medium on 480, which assumes M3.2 checks range centre-to-centre as MiningSystem does — edge-to-edge (hull half-sizes 110/45/30) shifts every figure by ~155 and leaves the 200-unit gap.

[R2-5] [Reviewer 2 Balance] [Major] [Balance]
Claim: Under symmetric play no attack is profitable: the defender's structural edge is the crossing time times the build rate — 5.7 fighters at two players (11.5 with ShipyardL2), 4.0 per front at four — which is larger than any economic edge the shipped numbers can produce, so matches end only by error and M3.11 Q3 is pre-answered "fifteen minutes".
Evidence: FACT — Layout.h:35 (12,000 units = 85.7 s and 8,485 = 60.6 s at 140 u/s), BuildSystem.h:108 (20 cr/s), Fighter 300 (Design.cpp:18-22, Catalog); §7 fighter v fighter 30 dps each; station 8,000 HP at hit value 300 = 12.5 dps per fighter; new ships spawn 155 units in front of the station (BuildSystem.cpp:101-118). INFERENCE (arithmetic on the tuning table under symmetric play): 86 s × 20 cr/s = 1,720 credits = 5.7 fighters built while a raid is in transit; a focus-fire fleet fight leaves the larger side √(A² − D²) survivors, so 12 v 9 leaves 7.9, the station then falls in 8,000/(12.5 × 7.9) = 81 s, and the five reinforcements the defender builds meanwhile die in 600/(30 × 7.9) = 2.5 s each and deal 380 damage in total — a 33% edge at contact wins in two minutes, and nothing in the shipped economy hands anyone 33%: under infinite ore both sides are slot-bound at 28 fighters (Part 1), under Q26's ore both have 7–10 fighters and 3–10 cr/s (Part 2). At four players the edge is 4.0 fighters on each of two 60.6-s fronts, which is why the 4p match is more resolvable, not less — the opposite of §3's worry.
Why it matters: P5 needs matches that end; M3.8 restarts only on victory; twenty stalemates are not answers.
Recommendation: a match clock — the host ends the match at tick 7,200 (360 s) and declares the higher station hull points the winner, tie broken by credits plus the catalog cost of live ships and modules (host-side facts; one comparison in M3.7's Victory.cpp) — so every M3.11 match yields a result in at most six minutes; and R2-4's depot as the intended symmetry breaker (the side holding one earns 25–47 cr/s against 4–12 — measured, Part 10 — a 2–3× fleet edge within 90 s of the cliff). Alternative: no clock; ANCHOR_RADIUS_UNITS 6,000 → 4,500 (crossing 64 s, edge 4.3 fighters) and station hull 8,000 → 5,000 (ten fighters kill it in 40 s), accepting that §7's 80–100 s window moves and every row built on 85.7 s is restated.
Confidence: Medium — the fleet-fight estimate ignores ring geometry and target selection (both unwritten), but the defender's transit edge is arithmetic and the harness shows no economic edge exists to beat it.

[R2-7] [Reviewer 2 Balance] [Minor] [Exploit]
Claim: A tap on an unaffordable build button cancels the item in progress, so the "save up" state Interface §6 shows as a lit button with a reddened cost destroys up to 20 s of slot progress on a mis-tap.
Evidence: FACT — BuildSystem.cpp:221-242 (Commit refunds the current item first, then `item = BuildItem{}; return Unaffordable` when the balance does not cover the new cost — "which is what a player who taps something they cannot afford has asked for"); Interface.md:426 ("an item you cannot yet pay for keeps its button lit and reddens its cost… every available button stays live while it builds, so a tap replaces what is in progress"); BuildSystem.cpp:63-81 (ShipyardL1 = 400 ticks).
Scenario / proof: a Miner at 140 of 150 ticks, 100 credits banked, Fighter tapped: refund 150 → 250 < 300 → Unaffordable, the miner is cancelled with 7 s lost and nothing building. The bank sits at 100–200 credits for the whole first minute in every harness table, which is where a 96 px button is hit under time pressure.
Why it matters: the one place the interface says "save up", the host answers "cancel".
Recommendation: in Commit test `credits + refund >= cost` before touching the item and, when it fails, return Unaffordable leaving the current item running (three lines; Q35's refund-first order is preserved for the affordable case). Alternative: dim, not redden, a button whose cost exceeds credits plus the current item's refund, on the client, from facts the per-player block already carries.
Confidence: High on the code; the frequency is INFERENCE.

[R2-8] [Reviewer 2 Balance] [Minor] [Balance]
Claim: With no per-rock throughput (D5), stacking miners on the best rock beats spreading by 21–46% of income, and Q26's "ten rocks keeps [six miners] from queuing on one" names a rule that must be written or deleted.
Evidence: FACT — MiningSystem.cpp:111-124 (extraction is per miner; no per-rock state); Generator.h:37-38; harness Part 1 stacked v spread: line K 66.5 v 45.6 cr/s (5,895 v 1,885 banked); Part 9: remapped K 23 fighters stacked v 19 spread.
Scenario / proof: ten miners on rocks 0 and 7 (797/816 units) earn 66.5 cr/s; spread over ten rocks, 45.6. With finite ore only the order of exhaustion changes.
Why it matters: a hidden 21–46% edge for the player who stacks, invisible to the one who spreads as the design invites; and the count of ten rocks is justified by a rule that does not exist.
Recommendation: one extractor per rock per tick — a per-rock "extracted this tick" flag in MiningSystem::Advance (slot order, deterministic); a second miner in range holds in Extracting with no cargo change (a queue with no new wire state); a rock then yields at most 20 ore/s and ten rocks serve about thirty miners, which makes Q26's sentence true. Alternative: delete the sentence in Q26 and Generator.h:37-38 and accept stacking, which finite ore turns into a timing difference only.
Confidence: High.

(c) Checked and found sound — protect these.
- Economy.cpp:24-33: the remainder is carried in hundredths of a thousandth, so a 100-ore hold is exactly 100 / 125 / 150 credits at 100/125/150% (Q56), and the processor percentage is read per delivery (Economy.cpp:26), so a processor built or lost mid-run takes effect on the next hold.
- BuildSystem::TicksForCost (BuildSystem.cpp:63-81) rounds up in one division: Miner 150 ticks, Fighter 300, Fighter at L1 200 and L2 150, ShipyardL1 267 at ×1.5 — exactly as Q47/Q56 state; the multiplier is fixed at order time (BuildSystem.cpp:143), so a shipyard completing mid-item neither speeds nor slows it, which is acceptable and stated.
