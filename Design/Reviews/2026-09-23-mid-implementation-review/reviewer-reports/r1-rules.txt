[R1-1] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Blocker] [Logic]
Claim: Because session tokens are drawn from a PRNG seeded by the match seed, a client presenting a token from a previous match or previous host run is "recognized" onto whichever client was seated first, and the two clients then evict each other on seat 1 every second while seat 2 stays empty — so a two-tablet match cannot start about half the times the host is restarted with the same seed.
Evidence: FACT. Sessions.cpp:8-14 (`Begin` reseeds `m_tokens = Pcg32{_matchSeed, TOKEN_STREAM}`), :70-84 (a token match wins before anything else, from any endpoint, and overwrites the session's endpoint), :106-118 (a fresh seat draws the next token from the same stream). SessionsTests.cpp:238-249 pins that two runs of one seed issue the same tokens; ADR-013:169-172 calls the repeat "the outcome anybody would want". App.cpp:525 reads the persisted token at launch and :640 writes it; ClientFrame.cpp:20-31 rejoins with the held token after 1,000 ms of silence; Host.cpp:178-183 drops every packet from an endpoint with no session. Server.cpp:224 defaults every run to DEFAULT_MATCH_SEED.
Scenario / proof: Reproduced with r1/tokens.cpp driving unmodified Sessions.cpp. Match 1: A = player 1 token b11fa2dc…, B = player 2 token ba4ef9dc…. Host restarted (or `BeginMatch`) with the same seed; B launches first: B → Accepted player 1, token b11fa2dc… (A's old token); A → Rejoined player 1, B's endpoint overwritten; `PlayerAt(B) = 0`, seats held 1. B hears nothing for 1 s, rejoins with b11fa2dc… → Rejoined player 1, now `PlayerAt(A) = 0`. They alternate; seat 2 is never taken. With A first, or with a different seed, both are seated correctly — the failure is arrival-order dependent.
Why it matters: The evening of twenty matches restarts the host per match today (M3.8 is unwritten) and each tablet carries the last match's token in LocalState; every second restart yields two clients flickering "Reconnecting" and no match. M3.8's reseed only avoids it if the new seed never repeats across host runs, and the first match after any server restart with the default seed is exposed regardless.
Recommendation: Salt the token stream with a per-host-run nonce taken at the seam: `Sessions::Begin(count, seed, nonce)` with `m_tokens = Pcg32{seed ^ nonce, TOKEN_STREAM}` (nonce = wall-clock milliseconds at `RunHost` start; a token is not simulation state, so R16's seam rule permits it; suites pass a constant). Delete `TwoRunsOfOneMatchIssueTheSameTokens`, rewrite ADR-013's "Tokens repeat" paragraph, and add a test "a token from a previous Begin never matches a seat of this one". Alternative: keep the seed-derived stream but put a 16-bit host-run nonce in the token's top bits and have `Admit` ignore any token whose nonce is not this run's.
Confidence: High (reproduced). Only a guarantee that no seed is ever reused across host runs would lower it to Major.

[R1-2] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Balance]
Claim: Under any per-tick integer damage rule — the obvious choice for M3.0 — stations and modules are immune to every ship weapon and the safe-zone kill time moves by ±35%; the plan's own analysis of the cadence gate stops at the 25→1 ship case and misses that the structure column goes to zero. (This deepens the map's RN item on F3/ADR-014; it does not re-report it.)
Evidence: FACT for the arithmetic: Catalog.cpp:71 (`MassDriver` 25 dps, range 600), :76 (`PointDefense` 60 dps, range 400); GameDesign §7 modifier table (70/60/25, 120/90/30) and the curve `base × 100 / (100 + 300)` = a quarter; §5's "ten fighters … about a minute" and §7's "two minutes per module, ten and a half minutes of one fighter"; Plan/M3-the-fight.md:31-35 computes only "25 ÷ 20 truncates to 1"; TechnicalDesign §2:98-100 orders movement, weapons, …, deaths, victory.
Scenario / proof: Per mount per tick at 20 Hz. MassDriver v Small: 17.5 dps = 0.875/tick → truncated 0 (a fighter cannot kill a miner) or rounded 1 (20 dps, +14%). MassDriver v station or module: 25 × 100/400 = 6.25 dps = 0.3125/tick → 0 under truncation AND under rounding — no ship weapon can ever damage a base, and §5's whole standoff argument is void. PD v Medium: 54 dps = 2.7/tick → 2 (80 dps, 7.5 s to kill a fighter) or 3 (120 dps, 5 s) against the designed "under six seconds" (5.56 s). A 1 Hz per-shot rule with integer damage per shot lands within +4…+9% of every row (17 → 14 s v miner; 15 → 20 s v fighter exact; 6 → 66.7 s for ten fighters on a station, 125 s for one on a module; 108 → 6 s for PD). Only the hundredths accumulator the plan lists as "an option" reproduces §7 exactly. Three further choices with balance effect are also unpinned: (i) deaths applied inside the weapons pass in slot order give the lower slot's shot first and let a ship killed earlier in the pass never fire that tick — a systematic player-1 edge in symmetric fights; (ii) at 1 Hz, whether the first shot fires on acquisition or after a full interval decides whether a ship leaving range takes 0 or 1 extra shot (ADR-004's "disengaging works" becomes phase luck); (iii) overkill when two shooters hit one target in one tick.
Why it matters: M3.11's balance answers are only trustworthy if the simulation does what §7's table says; a per-tick integer rule makes the station unkillable and a per-shot rule quietly moves every row.
Recommendation: ADR-014: accumulate damage per weapon in hundredths of a point per tick (`hundredths += dps × modifier × 100 / 20`, apply the whole points, keep the remainder), fire event every tick the weapon is in range (tracers may be thinned client-side), deaths applied at the deaths step after every weapon has fired, overkill allowed; pin the §7 rows as tests with a tolerance of one tick's damage. Alternative: 1 Hz shots with integer damage per (weapon, size class) from the table above, first shot immediate on acquisition, deaths deferred — and rewrite §7's rows to the +4…+9% figures rather than leaving them as continuous numbers.
Confidence: High (arithmetic).

[R1-3] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Coherence]
Claim: Miner flight as specified in M3.6 ("with no order") never applies to the miners §7's raid table assumes are fleeing, and the shipped mining state machine cannot express flight for an ordered miner without a new phase.
Evidence: FACT. GameDesign §7 flight paragraph: "A miner with no order that is fired upon flees"; §7 table row "Miners lost fleeing 1,500 units to the station: about 3½" — 1,500 units at the Miner's 100 u/s is 15 s, three fighters at 35 dps on 450 HP kill one every 4.29 s → 3.5: that row is only reachable by miners at the field, which are miners under a standing mine order. Plan/M3-the-fight.md:214-216: "a miner explicitly ordered to mine keeps mining". MiningSystem.cpp:93-109 (`HeadFor` re-issues the rock as the move destination on every ToOre tick), :111-124 (`Extracting` never re-checks range), :153-179 (`Unloading` never re-checks reach). Harness S3f: a flee written as a move order on a ToOre miner is overwritten in the same pass (destination back to the rock, 5 units moved); S3e: a miner in Extracting moved 496 units away keeps extracting (cargo 61,000 milli-ore at 2.5× the 200-unit range).
Scenario / proof: The only miners a raider can reach are at the rocks (600–1,500 out; PD covers 400); an idle miner sits at the spawn point 140 units from the station and never needs to flee. Under M3.6's condition, flight fires for miners that are already safe and never for the six the row was computed on; M3.6's exit test ("§7's figure … reproduced") would pass on a situation that does not occur in play.
Why it matters: §7 says flight is what gives the defender any counterplay without watching the right part of a 16,384-unit map; without it the M3.11 answer to "is the raid arithmetic right" measures a game §7 does not describe.
Recommendation: Rule: a miner with a mine order that is fired upon suspends the standing order and flees — add `MiningPhase::Fleeing` keeping `rock`; enter from ToOre/Extracting on being hit; return to ToOre when not fired on for 60 ticks or once within 400 of the acceptor; a player's move order still overrides. Make Extracting and Unloading re-check `Within` each tick and fall back to ToOre/ToUnload. Re-derive the §7 row under that rule (fighters at 140 u/s catching miners at 100 u/s keeps 3½). Alternative: keep §7's wording and define "no order" in the design as "no move order", which is the same rule stated once.
Confidence: High.

[R1-4] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Logic]
Claim: Once ships die, an order whose selection contains a ship that died since the client's last record is refused in its entirety and never acknowledged, so a retreat tapped during a raid silently does nothing for the survivors.
Evidence: FACT. CommandIntake.cpp:119-134 returns `StaleGeneration` before any identity is acted on and before `m_lastApplied` is written (:190-192); :66-69 gives the opposite rule ("the acknowledgment still advances on a refusal … the host understood") for station orders only; Q24 (OpenQuestions.md:99); CommandValidationTests.cpp:196 pins `AHalfValidSelectionAppliesNoneOfIt`; Selection.cpp:29-47 prunes only against records already received (App.cpp:862). Harness S2: Move {a, b, dead c} → rejection 5 (StaleGeneration), `LastAppliedSequence` 0, neither a nor b ordered.
Scenario / proof: §7's raid: three fighters on six miners kill one every ~4.3 s; the client learns of a death ≥ 50–150 ms after the host (replication, 75 ms interpolation delay, more under D7's loss). A retreat tap on the group inside that window carries the dead identity → the whole order is refused, no ack, the marker stays, and (R1-5) nothing resends. INFERENCE on frequency: ≈ 100 ms / 4,300 ms ≈ 2–3% of orders issued during a raid — precisely the orders the balance questions depend on.
Why it matters: The defender's counterplay fails exactly when it is exercised; Q24's rule was written when nothing died.
Recommendation: In `Apply`, drop identities that resolve to `NO_ENTITY` (a dead ship cannot be ordered; nothing is half-applied), refuse only `NotOwned` and `SelectionTooLong`, accept an all-dead selection as a no-op, and advance `m_lastApplied` on every refusal past the sequence check. Re-pin the test as "a foreign identity applies none; a dead one is skipped". Alternative: keep all-or-nothing but acknowledge refusals and add a one-byte refusal code to the player block so the client re-issues the order with the pruned selection.
Confidence: High on the mechanism (harness); Medium on frequency.

[R1-5] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Logic]
Claim: The packaged client sends every command exactly once; ADR-003's "repeated in every packet until acknowledged" reliability channel exists on the wire and in the Bot but not in the game, so a lost datagram is a lost order and its marker stays on screen until some later order is acknowledged.
Evidence: FACT. TechnicalDesign §4 (upstream): "repeated in every outgoing packet until acknowledged"; `FillOldestFirst` GameCore/Command.h:224-234. App.cpp:793-800, :892-901, :961-981 and :1021-1028 each build a new `CommandPacket` with that tap's command(s) and send it once; App.cpp:601 sends the 4 Hz view report with `commands = {}`; no outstanding list exists in App.cpp or ClientFrame (grep for `outstanding`/`FillOldestFirst`); only Bot/Bot.cpp:172 and GameClient/BotPolicy.cpp:113-139 implement it. OrderMarker.cpp:15-25 clears markers only by acknowledgment. Wireless loss is unmeasured (map §6: M0.5 owed).
Scenario / proof: At 1% loss and ~100 taps a match, one order per match vanishes silently — twenty per evening — and in a quiet phase its marker persists for the rest of the match.
Why it matters: Balance readings are contaminated by orders that never arrived, and the design's stated reliability property is false on the device it is for.
Recommendation: Move `BotPolicy`'s pattern into `ClientFrame`: keep `m_outstanding`, retire by `SequenceIsNewer` against `own->lastCommandSequenceApplied` in `DrainPackets`, and `FillOldestFirst` it into every outgoing packet including the 250 ms view report; drop a command older than 40 ticks and clear its marker so a permanently refused one cannot ride forever. Alternative: keep send-once but time markers out at 2 s so the interface stops asserting an order that was lost.
Confidence: High.

[R1-6] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Logic]
Claim: "Last station standing" has no answer when the last two stations die on the same tick, and the symmetric AI-versus-AI fixture makes that the expected outcome rather than a corner case, so the unattended match never ends.
Evidence: FACT. GameDesign §2: "Victory is the last station standing"; "A host with no clients keeps simulating"; "Four AI players and no human is … a test fixture". Plan/M3-the-fight.md:237-239 demands simultaneous destruction "resolves the same way every run" but names no rule; M3.10:322-324 "a match against it reaches a victory unattended". Layout.h:83-98 and Generator.cpp:224-250 make every region an exact copy.
Scenario / proof: INFERENCE. Two identical deterministic stub AIs on exactly mirrored halves launch on the same tick and complete identical ~64 s sieges on the same tick; with deaths deferred (R1-2's symmetric choice) both stations reach zero together → zero stations standing → the condition is never true → no victory, no restart, the fixture hangs. If deaths are applied in slot order instead, player 1 wins every mirror match by construction.
Why it matters: M3.10's exit criterion and M3.8's restart loop stall on the fixture; a human match hitting it (rare) runs on with two eliminated players.
Recommendation: §2: "If every remaining station is destroyed on one tick the match is a draw; a draw ends and restarts the match like a victory and the result overlay names it." Test in VictoryTests: kill both on one tick, assert the match ends. Alternative: rank by hull remaining at the start of the tick, ties a draw.
Confidence: High that the rule is missing; Medium on the fixture's exact simultaneity (it depends on R1-2's death-order choice).

[R1-7] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Major] [Logic]
Claim: The state hash covers identity, position, heading and hull *type* only, so hull points, cargo, mine phase, owner, credits and build items can diverge between builds without the determinism test or ADR-002's four-pair run noticing until a death or a build completion happens to shift.
Evidence: FACT. StateHash.cpp:62-69 folds `id`, `position`, `heading`, `static_cast<uint8_t>(entity.hull)`; Entity.h:93-94 marks the `HullId` as "HASHED"; TechnicalDesign §2 says "hull" without saying which. Credits, pending hundredths and items live outside the world (BuildSystem.h:208-209, Economy.h:49). Harness S1: setting hull 450→1, cargo 0→99,000, phase None→Unloading, owner 1→2 and a move order each leave the hash UNCHANGED. DeterminismTests.cpp:211-216 asserts only the hash plus loose bounds (`credits < 1000`, `ships ≥ 7`, `delivered ≥ 2,000,000`).
Scenario / proof: At M3 an x64-versus-ARM64 divergence in damage accumulation (an unsequenced expression of the kind Sessions.cpp:25-31 warns about, or a differently rounded intermediate) leaves every position identical until a death timing shifts; the owed MSVC four-pair run passes while combat diverges. Cargo differences reach the hash only through unload timing, credits only through a build's completion tick.
Why it matters: The one gate that protects R16 answers "no divergence" about the M3 state it cannot see.
Recommendation: Amend ADR-002: fold per entity `hullRemaining` (u16), `owner`, `MineOrder.phase`, `rock`, `cargoMilliOre`, `unloadTarget`, then per player credits (u32), pending hundredths (u64) and the build item (active, design, ticksElapsed, ticksRequired, creditsSpent, upgrade); re-pin the value; extend `EveryHashedFieldChangesIt`. Alternative: leave the ADR-002 hash as defined and assert a second economy/combat hash beside it in `TheScriptedMatchHashesToItsPinnedValue`.
Confidence: High.

[R1-8] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Minor] [Coherence]
Claim: M3.8b's exit criterion "killing a shipyard slows the queue already running" cannot be met, because the build system bakes the multiplier into `ticksRequired` when the item starts and never re-reads it.
Evidence: FACT. Plan/M3-the-fight.md:274-275; CommandIntake.cpp:72-74 ("an item keeps the rate it began at, so a shipyard finished or lost mid-build changes the next item and not this one"); BuildSystem.cpp:140-144, :212-218 (`ticksRequired` fixed at `Start`), :275-279 (one tick per tick). GameDesign §5 "The shipyard multiplies the rate" reads as continuous. Harness S4b.
Scenario / proof: Kill the enemy's shipyard one second into a ten-second Fighter build and it still finishes at ×1.5; build a shipyard while a Fighter is in progress and the Fighter waits the full 15 s.
Why it matters: A plan criterion and a code comment contradict each other; whichever an implementer follows, the other's test fails.
Recommendation: Make progress rate-based: `progressHundredths += BUILD_RATE × BuildRateMultiplierPercent(world, player)` per tick in `Advance`, complete when ≥ cost × 100, `WireProgressPercent` = progress / cost; Q56's round-up survives as "completes on the tick progress reaches cost". Alternative: keep the baked rate and rewrite M3.8b and §5 to "the rate is fixed when the item starts".
Confidence: High.

[R1-9] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Minor] [Coherence]
Claim: M3.4's criterion "the client evicts the entity from the removal list and never from absence" is contradicted by the shipped forget rule, and wrecks have no defined source under that rule.
Evidence: FACT. Plan/M3-the-fight.md:178-181; ReplicaStore.cpp:90-101 forgets after `FORGET_AFTER_SWEEPS × SweepTicks` = 3 ticks at 110 entities (Update.h:93-103); D7 (map) for the link-silence interaction.
Scenario / proof: M3.4's own test (an entity missing without a removal must not be treated as dead) fails against the store as built after four ticks. Wrecks: spawned on removals only, a 200 ms burst loss makes living ships vanish without wrecks and reappear; spawned on any eviction, the same burst litters the field with wrecks of living ships.
Why it matters: The M3.4 exit criterion is unimplementable as written, and the choice decides what a player sees during ordinary Wi-Fi loss.
Recommendation: Wrecks and "a dead ship leaves the selection" from removals only; forgetting is garbage collection for a rejoin — run it only after `Clear()`/while `m_reconnecting`, or raise the horizon above LINK_SILENCE (≥ 20 ticks) per D7; reword M3.4 to "never treated as dead by absence inside the forget horizon". Alternative: keep the 3-sweep forget and have the accumulator emit a removal for every entity it stops sending — heavier and pointless at MVP scale.
Confidence: High.

[R1-10] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Minor] [Logic]
Claim: An elimination produces 55 removals in one tick against a cap of 48 per update, and the pending list is served first-in-first-out, so the last seven ride no update for ten ticks.
Evidence: FACT. Update.h:64 (`MAX_REMOVALS_PER_UPDATE` 48), :86 (ten repeats); Accumulator.cpp:221-230 sends the first 48 in list order, :260-268 decrements only those sent. Target scale 50 ships + 4 modules + 1 station (map §0), removed in one tick by M3.7:237-238. Harness S5: 48 delivered at tick 4, the remaining 7 first sent at tick 14 (500 ms).
Scenario / proof: The loser's last seven ships linger half a second after the rest of the fleet vanished (at MVP scale they are forgotten by absence first — which R1-9 removes).
Why it matters: Cheap to fix now; the first mass death in M3 exposes it.
Recommendation: Serve pending removals never-sent-first (sort by `remaining` descending) or round-robin, so every removal leaves within ⌈N/48⌉ ticks. Alternative: raise the cap to 110 (330 B; the record floor drops from 65 to about 50 — re-run `Scripts/DatagramBudget.py`).
Confidence: High (measured).

[R1-11] [Reviewer 1 Rules] [Minor] [Coherence]
Claim: ADR-013's proposed detection of a reseed ("the snapshot's own tick going backwards") can never fire because `BeginMatch` does not reset the tick; the actual restart mechanism is a forced rejoin with a one-second blackout and arrival-order seating, and neither is written down.
Evidence: FACT. Host.cpp:32-58 resets world, intake, build, economy, sessions and accumulator but not `m_tick`; ADR-013:151-155; Sessions.cpp:10 clears seats → Host.cpp:178-183 drops the clients' packets → ClientFrame.cpp:20-31 rejoins after 1,000 ms; Sessions.cpp:106-118 seats by lowest free slot in arrival order. ReplicaStore.cpp:27-32 and :54-58 gate the own block and every record on the tick not going backwards.
Scenario / proof: At victory the host calls `BeginMatch`; both clients show "Reconnecting" for a second and rejoin; whoever arrives first is player 1, so sides can swap between matches. Anyone who "fixes" the detection by resetting `m_tick` freezes every client that did not `Clear()` its store.
Why it matters: M3.8 will otherwise be built on a detection that cannot happen.
Recommendation: State that the tick is monotonic across matches; add a `matchOrdinal` byte to `JoinReply` so a client knows it landed in a new match; have `BeginMatch` carry the previous match's token→player mapping so a recognized returning client keeps its side (which is what ADR-013:169-172 wanted and R1-1's salt would otherwise remove). Alternative: accept arrival-order seating and the one-second blackout and say so in M3.8.
Confidence: High on the facts; INFERENCE that players will mind the side swap.
