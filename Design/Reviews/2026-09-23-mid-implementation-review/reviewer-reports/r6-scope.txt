## (d) Findings

**[R6-1] [Reviewer 6 Scope] [Blocker] [Feasibility]**
Claim: A five-minute two-player match is not reachable with the tuning as written, so "twenty matches in an evening" is an unvalidated premise, not a plan.
Evidence: FACT — GameDesign §2 asserts "a match of about five minutes instead of twenty" with no derivation; map §3 constants: Station 8,000 HP, hit value 300 (§7: a quarter lands), MassDriver 25 dps × 2 mounts, Fighter 300 cr at 20 cr/s (15 s), 1,000 starting credits, opposed stations 12,000 apart = 85.7 s at 140 u/s; PD range 400 < MassDriver 600 (§5); victory is the last station only (§2); M3.11 Q3 already asks "or does it run to fifteen?".
Scenario / proof: one fighter lands 12.5 dps on a station → 640 s. All-in against a defender who does nothing: 3 fighters by 45 s (900 cr), arrive 131 s, 8,000 ÷ 37.5 = 213 s → victory at 344 s. Economic opening (4 miners in 30 s, income ≈ 20 cr/s from ~60 s, a fighter every 15 s): ~8 fighters by 150 s, arrive 236 s, 80 s of fire → ~316 s; with ShipyardL1 ≈ 300 s. Those are floors against a passive defender; INFERENCE — any defender who builds fighters (20 s per fighter-vs-fighter, §7) pushes a symmetric match to 8–15 minutes. Second, the siege §5 describes needs the 400 < d ≤ 600 standoff band: 200 units = 12 authored px at 17.1 u/px (M2.13), narrower than a ten-Frigate ring formation (ring 1 six slots + ring 2 at 90-unit spacing ≈ 180-unit radius, RingAssignment/map §3), so part of a tapped-into-place siege lands inside point defense (2 × 60 dps × 90% vs 600 HP ≈ 5.6 s) unless an attack order halts at weapon range.
Why it matters: 20 × ~12 min + restarts ≈ 4.5 h, so the owner gets ~10 matches an evening and M3.11's answers come from half the sample the design counted on; every §7 figure M3.1's tests pin is pinned to a match that does not happen.
Recommendation: before M3.1 pins §7, add "time to kill a passive station with N fighters" as a §7 row and choose a starting value that makes it ≤ ~2 min for six fighters — hit value 300 → 100 (half lands; six fighters kill in 53 s) or HP 8,000 → 4,000 — and specify in M3.2 that an attack order pursues to weapon range and stops, so the standoff is automatic. Alternative: keep the numbers, restate §10/M3.11 as "ten matches an evening, two evenings", and add a scripted rush to the scratchpad harness the day M3.2 lands (build N fighters, attack, count ticks to victory) so the length is measured before the first evening.
Confidence: High on the floor (arithmetic on shipped constants); Medium on 8–15 min (no combat code exists yet). A scripted rush run after M3.2 would settle it.

**[R6-4] [Reviewer 6 Scope] [Major] [Logic]**
Claim: As written, miner flight (M3.6) never triggers for a miner that is actually mining, because every productive miner is under a standing mine order and flight requires "no order".
Evidence: FACT — GameDesign §4: "A miner told to mine keeps mining until it is told to do something else" (the design's only standing order); §7: "A miner with no order that is fired upon flees to its station"; M3-the-fight.md:206-223: "**With no order** is the condition and it matters — a miner explicitly ordered to mine keeps mining"; §7's table: "Miners lost fleeing 1,500 units ... about 3½" of six, against "all six" in the "was" column.
Scenario / proof: three fighters reach a home field where six miners are extracting under mine orders; none flees; §7's "was" row (all six, 25.7 s) is what M3.11 observes, and Q1 records the raid as a slaughter caused by an order-state rule rather than by a number.
Why it matters: M3.6 is the defender's only counterplay the design credits (§7: "without them the defender must be watching the right part of a 16,384-unit map"), and M3.11's answer on the axis §7 argues hardest would be wrong.
Recommendation: suppression is a *move or attack* order only; a mine order does not suppress flight; a fleeing miner keeps its `MineOrder` and cargo (M2.6 already keeps cargo on abandon) and resumes it when no hostile has been within MassDriver range plus a margin for N ticks — one register row for N. Alternative: cut M3.6, restore "all six" in §7 as the MVP truth, and let M3.11 test the slaughter honestly.
Confidence: High — three documents agree on the condition; the resume rule is the part that needs the register.

## (e) Checked and found sound

- **Risk ordering as a principle, executed in M0**: the loopback gate before the renderer, the world-scale gate before any renderer work; both closed with answers (`-a` alone; 1:1 ships). Protect the principle by applying it inside M3 (R6-9).
- **"An agent never marks a gate done"** holds in git: every gate-closing annotation is the owner's; the one agent-written gate line records an owner ruling.
- **Honest claims per step**: every M2 annotation ends "compiled and run under g++ ... not under MSVC ... not drawn", and CI's first MSVC compile of M1.14c found and fixed two tests (792fd78) — the protocol catches what it says it catches.
- **The composition model has paid for itself**: M2.9 was "smaller than written" because modules fell out of the catalog with the derivation untouched; the miner and the station needed no special case.
- **The one-second gates** (CheckDeterminism, CheckDesign, CheckProjectFiles, CheckHudGeometry): CheckDesign's plan-count recomputation caught M2's "eleven vs fifteen"; keep all four in CI exactly as they are.
- **ADR-024 at the MVP's scale**: every entity every tick with the second update, the sweep guarantee, and an accumulator that costs 16–75 µs at 2–4 players; the state hash held through the replication replacement (5aff739, all four pairs).
- **The restart already falls out of what is built**: `BeginMatch` drops every seat (ADR-013:147) and the M1.14 rejoin path re-seats a client and hands it the new seed; M3.8 needs only the result overlay and a cleared store. Protect that path from a "cleverer" detection.
- **The register discipline "asked before the code"**: Q51, Q52, Q54–Q57 were ruled before M2.6 and M2.11 were written; Q47 is the one exception and says so.
- **The Bot as an unpackaged client** (ADR-022): the cheapest second seat and the cheapest wireless probe the project has; its linkability outside a package must be protected.
- **M3.3's wire half is already in place** (`FireEvent`, `NoteFire`, the repeat count, the tests): do not rebuild it at M3.3.
- **Identity-ordered ties and the determinism script that grows with each system** (mining at M2.6, income at M2.7, a fight at M3.2, the AI at M3.10): keep every new system on that script; it is the only thing that makes the four-pair run worth its cost.
