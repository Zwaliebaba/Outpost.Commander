# ADR-010 — Ships path on a visibility graph, steer by heading, and part without colliding

Status: **accepted** · 2026-09-30

## Context

Design §9 says ships path around asteroid obstacles and hold a loose formation at the pace of the slowest ship when moved as a group, and that they avoid overlapping but do not collide physically. Design §4 makes every obstacle a circle on a plane. Implementation plan task 2.4 builds that in `GameLogic`, with provisional footprint radii and turn rates held as data until gate G5 sets ship sizes. Q4 asks that a tick take at most 5 ms with 200 ships (design §3). ADR-009 makes the tick the only clock and allows floats, since a replay only has to reproduce on the same binary.

## Decision

1. **Paths come from a visibility graph over grown circles.** `Outpost::Pathfinder` grows every obstacle by the ship's footprint radius plus a 0.5 m margin and rings each grown circle with a polygon whose edges touch it: 16 corners, or 8 for a grown circle no more than 60 m across in radius, such as a structure's, which keeps the polygon within 5 m of it. Two corners are joined when the line between them touches both polygons at those corners and nothing blocks it. A shortest path round convex obstacles bends only at corners and leaves each one along such a line, since a line that cuts into a polygon could be shortened, so the other pairs are never tested: whether a line touches a polygon is a few multiplications, and whether anything blocks it is a pass over the obstacles (task 4.2, [ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md)). A path is the shortest route through that graph from the ship to its goal, found by A* with the straight-line distance. When the straight line is clear, the path is the goal alone. On a map of circles this gives shortest paths without a grid, and the graph has a few hundred corners on the 2.3 map. The search checks lazily whether the start sees a corner: each corner is first given the straight line from the start, and the line is tested only when the corner comes off the queue. Testing every corner up front was most of a search's cost.
2. **One graph per footprint radius, built when match setup is over and kept as the structures change.** The graph depends only on the obstacles and the radius. `InProcessServer` builds one for each hull's radius and the Constructor's once match setup has placed its structures ([ADR-032](ADR-032-order-ticks.md) decision 1), and keeps them, so an order pays only for its search. Structures block movement (ADR-016): placing one extends the graphs over it, and destroying one takes it out of them ([ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md)). Each is brought up to date by the next path that needs its radius, or on a quiet tick (ADR-032 decision 2). A ship whose remaining path a new structure blocks searches again at once. The graphs are a cache: `Simulation`'s equality ignores them.
3. **A goal inside an obstacle moves to its edge.** A ship ordered onto an asteroid stops beside it, and a goal beyond the map's edge is pulled inside it.
4. **A group moves as a formation, along one route.** A move order for several ships lays a grid of slots around the destination, facing the way the group travels, with three footprint radii of the group's widest ship between neighbors. The ships that are ahead now take the front slots, so few paths cross. The group searches once, from its ships' mean position to the destination at its widest ship's clearance (`Outpost::GroupRoutes`, decided by the owner on 2026-09-30). Each ship goes straight to its slot when it can. Otherwise it joins that route at the furthest corner it can see and follows it, through the destination when the route's end does not see its slot. On a Move or attack-move order it follows a lane of its own beside the route's corners ([ADR-047](ADR-047-a-fight-seen-whole.md) decision 1). Only a ship that sees no route, or whose way along one would be more than 1.5 times the straight line to its slot, searches for itself. Its path then becomes a route the ships after it may join, so ships cut off from the group together pay for one search. The limit matters because every ship cruises at its path's length divided by the time the slowest ship needs for its own path, so all arrive together and none goes faster than it can: one ship's detour would slow the whole group. An order for more than 32 ships plans its paths over two ticks, and its ships set off together in the second (ADR-032 decision 3). A single ship is a group of one and searches for itself.
5. **Ships steer by heading, and turn in arcs** ([ADR-039](ADR-039-ships-turn-in-arcs.md)). Each tick a ship turns toward its next waypoint at its turn rate and moves the way it faces, slowing into a turn. It never turns on the spot.
6. **Corners are passed loosely and slots exactly.** A ship drops the next corner of its path once it is within its own radius of it, or can see the waypoint after it. A corner of its lane it drops at sight only from within three of its footprint radii ([ADR-047](ADR-047-a-fight-seen-whole.md) decision 2). Only the last waypoint, its slot, has to be reached. A ship that has tried to move for a second without getting 0.1 m closer to its waypoint gives it up: it passes a corner, or counts itself arrived at its slot. A ship coming about is not stalled (ADR-039 decision 4). This is what ends every jam: ships crowding round the same corner, and slots that an obstacle or the edge pushed onto each other.
7. **Ships part, they do not collide.** After moving, each pair of overlapping ships is pushed apart along the line between their centers, in identifier order. Two moving ships, or two idle ones, part half each. A ship standing to fire keeps its range (design §7, decided by the owner on 2026-10-01): pushed, it gives way only sideways round its target, half the overlap, never toward it or away, and a moving ship that pushed it takes the rest. Pushed straight at its target, it steps to the side its identifier picks. An idle ship holds its ground against a moving one, which slides round it. A group closing on an enemy so spreads its front into an arc at range, and its rear ships find their own places on it. Before, half-each pushing let a group's rear rows shove its front into the enemy: a group of Lances ended 60 m inside its 220 m range. Then a ship inside an obstacle leaves it by the shortest way, and a ship past the edge is pulled back. There is no momentum and no damage. Checking every pair is O(n²), which is 20,000 pairs at 200 ships.
8. **Movement numbers are data.** Each hull has `footprintRadiusMeters` and `turnRateDegreesPerSecond`, and each drive a `turnRateFactor`, in `OutpostCommander/Assets/Tuning.json` (ADR-008). The footprints are the hulls' sizes, decided with gate G5 on 2026-10-01: Small 8 m, Medium 14 m, Large 24 m. The turn rates are final too (owner, 2026-10-01). `MovementFor` combines them with the hull's speed and the drive's speed factor. The server refuses to start when a hull's footprint is wider than the map's minimum gap (ADR-008, task 2.3), because such a ship could be walled off.

## Consequences

- **Measured cost.** Task 2.7 measured the order ticks on the development machine at up to 8.3 ms, against Q4's 5 ms, with one search per ship (design §3). That is why the group now searches once. The figures below compare the two ways on task 2.7's load: 200 ships and 40 structures, with both 100-ship fleets ordered across the map in one tick, 20 times. They ran in a Linux container built with clang 18 at `-O2`, not on the development machine, so read them as a ratio. The development machine's figures come from the owner's `--measure --load` run.
  - One search per ship: a mean of 3.1–4.1 ms per order tick over three runs.
  - The group's route, with the lazy search: 0.8–1.3 ms, about a quarter.
  - The load's fleets are scattered over half the map, which is the worst case for a shared route: a clump sees its route. About one ship in ten still searches for itself.
  - In both, all 200 ships arrived within 60 s of the last order, and no two overlapped. A first version that also pulled hidden slots to the near side of an obstacle was faster still, but left ships jostling at the obstacle's edge, so slots stay where the grid puts them.
  - Building a graph for one radius on the map, with the two Command Stations, takes 0.25–0.4 ms, measured the same way.
- **A very large group's formation outgrows the space.** Two hundred ships make a grid about a kilometer across. Slots past the edge or inside obstacles are moved clear, some land on each other, and the stall rule settles those ships near their slots. The formation is loose, as the design asks, and the ships do not overlap.
- **Arrival is a position, not a promise.** A ship that stalls "arrives" where it stopped, up to about a footprint from its slot.
- **A structure destroyed cost a graph build per radius in use** (task 4.2), and now costs a reduction of each, as one placed costs an extension (ADR-054). With task 3.7's stress scene, 53 obstacles in all, one graph took 14–17 ms when every pair of corners was tested, which put a 34 ms tick into the scene each time a structure died. Keeping only the lines that touch both polygons, and eight corners round small circles, brought it to 1.4–2.8 ms with the same paths: the stress test fires as many shots and destroys as many ships either way. Measured in the Linux container as above, on `Pathfinder::Prepare` for each radius. The builds wait for quiet ticks where they can (ADR-032 decision 2).

## What this forecloses

- Grid or navigation-mesh pathing, flow fields and steering behaviors such as boids, without a new ADR.
- Physical collision between ships: ships part, but they do not bounce or damage each other.
- Formation shapes other than the grid, or formations that turn with the group on the way. The grid faces the direction of travel when the order is given.
