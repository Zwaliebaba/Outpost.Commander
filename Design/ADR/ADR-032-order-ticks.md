# ADR-032 — Graphs are built off the order, a large order plans over two ticks, and a search does less

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-03

## Context

Q4 asks that no tick take more than 5 ms with 200 ships and 40 structures, the orders included (Phase 1 design §10). The MVP measured order ticks at 5.7–8.7 ms on the development machine, and 20.7 ms on the first (MVP §3). Task 8.1 named the parts of a tick; task 8.2 measures them and fixes what they show.

**What an order tick spends.** Measured in a Linux container with clang 18 at `-O2`, on task 2.7's load (200 ships, 40 structures, both fleets of 100 ordered across the map every 200 ticks, 12 orders over 2,400 ticks), timed with 8.1's parts and counted with callgrind. The container is not the development machine: the MVP's figures are about 3.3 times these, on both the first and the later order ticks, so read them as ratios.

- **The first order tick, 6.5–10 ms, is graph builds: 5.2–7.8 ms of it.** `InProcessServer` built the four graphs, one per hull and the Constructor, and match setup then placed the bases' Command Stations, and in a measurement run the load's 40 structures. Each placement dropped the graphs, so the match's first order built all four. That is the MVP's 20.7 ms.
- **Later order ticks, 1.5–2.6 ms, are the ships' paths.** About one ship in three still makes a full search, some 60 a tick, and an order tick makes 6,000–11,000 line tests against 54 obstacles. By instructions: line tests 44% (a ship trying the routes of the ships before it 19%, graph builds 14%, searches 10%), the searches' priority queue 15%, and a graph build's own pass over pairs of corners 17%.
- **Parting ships is not it**: 0.07–0.2 ms a tick, order or not. The spatial grid design §10 names for it is not needed.

## Decision

1. **The graphs are built when match setup is over**, by `InProcessServer::PreparePathfinding` at the end of `CreateInProcessServer`, after the bases and any load are placed. The first order of a match finds them built.
2. **A tick that plans no paths builds again one graph the obstacles' last change dropped,** or extends one over the obstacles added since ([ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md)). The pathfinder remembers every clearance it has built a graph for; `Pathfinder::PrepareNext` builds or extends the first that needs it. A structure placed or destroyed in play so costs one graph's build or extension a tick, on the quiet ticks after it, instead of all of them in the next order. The graphs are a cache: when one is built changes no path. An order that comes before they are all built still builds what it needs, as before.
3. **An order for more than 32 ships plans its paths over two ticks.** It lays its slots and searches its group's route in its own tick, plans every other ship's path then, and the rest at the start of the next, before that tick's orders. Alternate ships rather than the front half, because the ships behind search more often: each tick then plans about half the work. Its ships hold, with no order, until every path is planned, and the group sets off together in the second tick at one pace, as ADR-010 decision 4 has it. The routes the first tick's searches found are kept for the second, so a ship there joins them as it would have. A ship given any other order in between leaves the group; one destroyed is skipped; an attack whose target is destroyed leaves its ships holding. The orders being planned are state, so `Simulation`'s equality compares them. An order of 32 ships or fewer plans and sets off in its own tick exactly as before.
4. **A search does less, for the same answer.** It no longer heaps every corner the start might see: those entries wait in a list whose smallest are sorted 64 at a time, and the next entry is the smaller of the list's front and the queue's, which takes them in the very order one queue would. And a ship tries a route only when the shortest any way along it could be, the line to one of its corners plus the route from there plus the line from its end, is within the detour limit; a route that is not would have been rejected after its line tests anyway.

## Consequences

- **Measured the same way, on the same load with the graphs built after setup:** the worst order tick goes from 2.58 ms to 1.24 ms, taking each tick's least time over five runs, since single runs in the container spike. The first order tick goes from 6.5–10 ms to 0.5–1.0 ms. The instructions an order's planning takes fall from 64.7 million to 51.9 million over seven pairs of orders: the split costs about 7% more in all, since the second half plans from where its ships then stand, sorting in batches saves 10%, and passing over hopeless routes 13%. At the ratio above, that is about 4.2 ms on the development machine against 8.7 ms, and an ordinary tick in place of 20.7 ms. The owner's `--measure --load` run is what records it.
- **Unchanged where it should be.** With every order of 32 ships or fewer, a run of 2,400 ticks ends in exactly the same state, bit for bit, as before this change, with the graphs built after setup or not. Decisions 2 and 4 change no state at all: the same load split into large orders ends in the same state with and without them.
- **Changed where it must be.** A large group sets off a tick later than before, 50 ms, and its second half plans from where its ships stand then. Q5 measures the first ship of a small order, which is unchanged; a large order's first visible response comes at most 50 ms later than before, within Q5's 150 ms of the MVP's measured 70 ms worst. The Q2 check is played by groups of up to 137 ships; its verdicts before and after are below.
- **The Q2 check passes all four criteria before and after**, run in full in the container, 6 minutes each with four threads. Its details move: the groups set off a tick later and plan from a moment later, so some battles end differently, and the shares of what is worth building shift by a few points at most budgets. Two changes are more than that. At 12,000 Ore under spread fire the Medium hull, which was not worth building there, now is, in M+I+La at 21%; and the starting stage at 4,500 Ore under spread fire is now an even three-way of S+I+MD, M+I+MD and M+I+La where it was M+I+MD and S+I+La with S+I+MD at 7%. Every recorded counter of the MVP's §12 still holds, which `BalanceCheckTests.TheRecordedCountersHold` checks at 2,000 Ore: those armies are under 32 ships and do not change at all. Whether the shift matters is the owner's to say.
- **Tried and not kept**, because the instruction counts said no: a uniform grid of obstacles for every line test, which on these long lines and 54 obstacles cost 6% more than testing every obstacle's box; and the obstacles' grown radii kept per clearance, 7% more. A grid for short lines and a graph's corners only is ADR-054's.
- **A graph a destroyed structure drops is built whole.** On the 2.3 km map one takes about 1.3 ms in the container, about 4 ms on the development machine. A placed structure extends the graphs instead, and the figures on the 5 km map are ADR-054's.
- **Combat ticks** that destroy a structure now leave its graphs to the quiet ticks after. An order in the few ticks after a structure falls can still build one, as before.

## What this forecloses

- Planning a large order over more than two ticks, without a new decision: a third tick would put its first response past Q5's 150 ms.
- Starting a large group's front ships before its rear ships are planned: the group keeps one pace, and its pace needs every path.
