# ADR-026 — A destroyed ship or structure breaks into its own triangles in a blast of particles, after DeepSpaceOutpost

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-02
Owns: explosion-particles, explosion-shards, glow-sprite, blast-colors, frame-triangle-draws, effect-random

## Context

The explosions of ships and structures follow the particle manager and the explosion manager of DeepSpaceOutpost, the Darwinia-derived sibling tree (owner, 2026-10-01). That code cannot move across. It draws through an OpenGL emulation layer in immediate mode, it rests on Darwinia's vector, array and global-state types, and it steps everything per server slice against a heightmap. What carries over is how it looks and its numbers.

DeepSpaceOutpost has two parts. `ParticleSystem` draws camera-facing quads of one texture with additive blending and no depth write. A building's death calls `Location::Bang`, which throws a fireball of large "core" particles and a few "debris" particles, and each piece of debris leaves "rocket trail" smoke as it flies. `ExplosionManager` breaks a shape's own triangles off. Each one flies from the shape's center at three times its distance from it a second, and drag in the square of its speed slows it. It tumbles on one of five shared spins, and the whole explosion fades out over 5 s. A building breaks three times over.

The particles are drawn with DeepSpaceOutpost's texture (owner, 2026-10-02). `Particle.dds` is its 16×16 `particle.bmp`: a flat square at 156 of 255 with a one-texel rim at 231 to 255 and full alpha (read texel by texel from `OutpostCommander/Assets/Textures/Particle.dds`), which DeepSpaceOutpost draws as a diamond, magnified without smoothing. That is a hard-edged, blocky look a soft spot does not have. Shards darken and shrink rather than fade with transparency. The textures are the owner's own.

## Decision

1. **`Outpost::ParticleSystem` in `GameApp` makes glows (ADR-019), drawn with DeepSpaceOutpost's particle texture.** `GlowPipeline` draws what DeepSpaceOutpost's particles are: additive camera-facing quads that are tested against depth but write none. It takes an optional sprite, as `StarPipeline` does (ADR-022). With one, `GlowSpritePS.hlsl` multiplies the glow's color by the texel, taken from sRGB to linear. It samples with no smoothing when magnified and, when minified, linearly within the nearest of the mip levels `BuildMipLevels` makes (`D3D12_FILTER_MIN_LINEAR_MAG_MIP_POINT`). `GameClient` keeps a second `GlowPipeline` with `Particle.dds` for the particles, while the exhausts stay soft spots. It draws them as diamonds, DeepSpaceOutpost's square turned an eighth of a turn, by laying each quad along the sum and the difference of the screen's right and up, halved, so a particle's radius reaches its tips. `Particle.dds` is deployed with the package. A particle has a kind, a birth tick, a start, a velocity, a radius and a color. Its place is a closed form of its age, start + velocity × (1 − e^(−friction × age)) / friction, the limit of DeepSpaceOutpost's per-step friction. What is drawn therefore depends only on the view's tick, as the shots' effects do, never on the frame rate. A particle keeps full brightness for three quarters of its life and then fades linearly. The two kinds are DeepSpaceOutpost's `ExplosionCore` and `ExplosionDebris`, with its lives and friction. Their colors are linear and scaled by its 90/255, because a glow's color includes its brightness; which colors they are is decision 8's.
2. **Debris trails are drawn from the debris, not stored.** Every 0.1 s of flight, DeepSpaceOutpost's server advance, a piece of debris may leave a puff of smoke with a 30% chance. The puff is half the debris's size and moves at a fifth of its speed at that moment. Whether a step leaves a puff is drawn from the debris's seed and the step, so it is the same in every frame. No puff is ever an entry of its own.
3. **`ParticleSystem::AddBlast` is `Bang`, sized by what blew up.** A blast reaches 1.5 times the footprint radius. Its fireball, debris and speeds are shares of that reach rather than DeepSpaceOutpost's units. A structure also throws the fast flash of `Building::Destroy`. Space has no ground and no down, so gravity, the upward throw and bouncing are left out. Velocities spread in every direction, half as far up and down as across the plane the camera looks at.
4. **`Outpost::ExplosionManager` in `GameApp` breaks the model's own triangles.** `GameClient` keeps every model's `MeshData` on the CPU as well as in video memory. It gives the manager the mesh, the pose the view drew it at and the color it was drawn in, the faces' ([ADR-040](ADR-040-lines-over-dark-faces.md) decision 4); a mesh breaks whole, its parts as they stand at rest ([ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md) decision 5). Each triangle with an area, and with edges adding up to at least 5% of the explosion's radius, becomes a shard: its center, its corners about it and its normal, the placed sum of its corners' normals; a triangle whose normals cancel is dropped. The shard flies from the mesh's center at five times its distance a second, plus up to 0.5 radii a second in any direction (owner, 2026-10-02). The scatter stands in for the upward throw. Drag in the square of the speed gives distance = ln(1 + k·s·t) / k with k = 1.5 / radius, so a shard from the rim flies about 2.1 radii in its life, computed from those constants. A shard turns about the axis of one of five spins per copy, with rotational friction 0.2. An explosion lasts 3 s, darkens linearly to black, and shrinks each shard to nothing over its last 40%. A structure breaks three times over, as a DeepSpaceOutpost building does, and a ship once. One explosion keeps at most 400 shards, all copies together, chosen at random as DeepSpaceOutpost's fraction does.
5. **`Neuron::MeshPipeline::DrawTriangles` draws geometry made on the CPU each frame.** It copies a triangle list, already in the world, into this frame's slot of an upload buffer and draws it with the pipeline's root signature and state, as one instance with an identity world matrix ([ADR-053](ADR-053-instanced-meshes.md)). Each frame in flight has one slot of 65,536 vertices (`MAX_FRAME_VERTICES`), 1.75 MiB at 28 bytes a vertex, which `DrawLineList` shares ([ADR-042](ADR-042-faint-footprint-lines.md) decision 4). A call that would overflow the slot, or finds the frame's instances taken, draws nothing and returns false. The pipeline is opaque and culls back faces, so each shard is drawn twice, once each way round with its normal turned. Each explosion is one draw in its darkened color, newest first, so a frame too busy for every shard drops the oldest.
6. **`GameClient` starts both from the snapshot, before the view takes it.** `DestroyedView` does not say which structure blew up. The client looks the entity up in the newest snapshot it already holds, puts it where the server says it died, and places and colors its model as it drew it. Both play from one tick before the snapshot, as a shot does (ADR-013). The seed is the entity's identifier and the tick, so one destruction always looks the same. The particles have their own `GlowPipeline` and so their own 4,096 instances a frame; past that a frame drops particles, never exhausts. `CombatEffects` draws shots only.
7. **`Outpost::EffectRandom` is the client's randomness for how things look:** the explosions', and the asteroid fields' layout ([ADR-087](ADR-087-fields-of-their-own.md) decision 1). It is SplitMix64, like the sky's, and it is presentation, not the simulation's `Random` (ADR-009). Every draw is its own statement, so a seed gives the same draws on every compiler.
8. **A blast runs from white-yellow to amber and is never in a side's color, and its fireball is born hot.** The fireball's puffs are between (0.42, 0.37, 0.23) and (0.4, 0.24, 0.08) in linear color, each born at (0.55, 0.52, 0.44), near white and half as bright again, and cooling to its own color over its first 0.4 s. The debris is between (0.22, 0.13, 0.04) and (0.337, 0.22, 0.08), and the smoke is gray. So a ship blowing up in its own fleet never reads as the enemy's.

## Consequences

- **The frame gains a draw call for the particles, and one per explosion on screen and its upload.** The heaviest case is the slot full: 65,536 vertices a frame, about 27 explosions of 400 shards, computed from the constants, not measured. The frame's budget is in [`Design/Budgets.md`](../Budgets.md).
- **Every size, speed, color and count is a constant** at the top of `ParticleSystem.cpp` and `ExplosionManager.cpp`, in DeepSpaceOutpost's proportions.
- **`GameAppTests` checks the math without a GPU.** `ParticleSystemTests` checks timing, seeding, friction, fading, trails, sizing and the cap. `ExplosionManagerTests` checks timing, both faces, drag, darkening and shrinking, placement, seeding, the shard cap, copies, and triangles too small to see. `CombatEffectsTests` checks that a destruction is not its job.

## What this forecloses

- Shards that fade with transparency. That would need a blended pipeline with sorting; a shard darkens and shrinks instead.
- Gravity, ground and bouncing particles, which space does not have.
