# ADR-026 — A destroyed ship or structure breaks into its own triangles in a blast of particles, after DeepSpaceOutpost

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-02

## Context

Task 3.5 showed a destruction as a flat disc that swelled and darkened, with a ring running out past it, drawn by the mesh pipeline. On 2026-10-01 the owner asked for the particle manager and the explosion manager of DeepSpaceOutpost, the Darwinia-derived sibling tree, for the explosions of ships and structures. That code cannot move across. It draws through an OpenGL emulation layer in immediate mode, it rests on Darwinia's vector, array and global-state types, and it steps everything per server slice against a heightmap. What carries over is how it looks and its numbers.

DeepSpaceOutpost has two parts. `ParticleSystem` draws camera-facing quads of one soft texture with additive blending and no depth write. A building's death calls `Location::Bang`, which throws a fireball of large red "core" particles and a few "debris" particles, and each piece of debris leaves "rocket trail" smoke as it flies. `ExplosionManager` breaks a shape's own triangles off. Each one flies from the shape's center at three times its distance from it a second, and drag in the square of its speed slows it. It tumbles on one of five shared spins, and the whole explosion fades out over 5 s. A building breaks three times over.

The owner settled four questions on 2026-10-01. This is a new task after the MVP, in one PR. Particles were to use the analytic glow first, on the belief that `Particle.dds` was a soft dot that a glow already matches. It is not: it is DeepSpaceOutpost's 16×16 `particle.bmp`, a flat square at 156 of 255 with a one-texel rim at 231 to 255 and full alpha, which DeepSpaceOutpost draws as a diamond, magnified without smoothing. That is a hard-edged, blocky look a soft spot does not have, and on 2026-10-02 the owner asked for the texture. Shards darken and shrink rather than fade with transparency. The textures are the owner's own.

## Decision

1. **`Outpost::ParticleSystem` in `GameApp` makes glows (ADR-019), drawn with DeepSpaceOutpost's particle texture.** `GlowPipeline` already draws what DeepSpaceOutpost's particles are: additive camera-facing quads that are tested against depth but write none. It gains an optional sprite, as `StarPipeline` has (ADR-022). With one, `GlowSpritePS.hlsl` multiplies the glow's color by the texel, taken from sRGB to linear. It samples with no smoothing when magnified and linearly from the mip levels `BuildMipLevels` makes when minified. `GameClient` keeps a second `GlowPipeline` with `Particle.dds` for the particles, while the exhausts stay soft spots. It draws them as diamonds, DeepSpaceOutpost's square turned an eighth of a turn, by laying each quad along the sum and the difference of the screen's right and up, halved, so a particle's radius reaches its tips. `Particle.dds` is deployed with the package, as `starburst.dds` is. A particle has a kind, a birth tick, a start, a velocity, a radius and a color. Its place is a closed form of its age, start + velocity × (1 − e^(−friction × age)) / friction, the limit of DeepSpaceOutpost's per-step friction. What is drawn therefore depends only on the view's tick, as task 3.5's effects do, never on the frame rate. A particle keeps full brightness for three quarters of its life and then fades linearly. The two kinds are DeepSpaceOutpost's `ExplosionCore` and `ExplosionDebris`, with its lives and friction. Their colors are linear and carry its 90/255 alpha, because a glow's color includes its brightness. DeepSpaceOutpost's red gave way to a white-yellow flash and amber debris, so that a blast is never read as the Tarkan's red (ADR-028).
2. **Debris trails are drawn from the debris, not stored.** Every 0.1 s of flight, which was DeepSpaceOutpost's server advance, a piece of debris may leave a puff of smoke with a 30% chance. The puff is half the debris's size and moves at a fifth of its speed at that moment. Whether a step leaves a puff is drawn from the debris's seed and the step, so it is the same in every frame. No puff is ever an entry of its own.
3. **`ParticleSystem::AddBlast` is `Bang`, sized by what blew up.** A blast reaches 1.5 times the footprint radius. Its fireball, debris and speeds are shares of that reach rather than DeepSpaceOutpost's units. A structure also throws the fast flash of `Building::Destroy`. Space has no ground and no down, so gravity, the upward throw and bouncing are left out. Velocities spread in every direction, half as far up and down as across the plane the camera looks at.
4. **`Outpost::ExplosionManager` in `GameApp` breaks the model's own triangles.** `GameClient` keeps every model's `MeshData` on the CPU as well as in video memory, which is about 1 MB across the 29 models. It gives the manager the mesh, the pose the view drew it at and the color it was drawn in. Each triangle with an area, and with edges adding up to at least 5% of the explosion's radius, becomes a shard: its center, its corners about it and its face normal. The shard flies from the mesh's center at five times its distance a second, plus up to 0.5 radii a second in any direction. The scatter stands in for the upward throw. Both are faster than DeepSpaceOutpost's, which the owner found too slow for a ship to read as blowing apart (2026-10-02). Drag in the square of the speed gives distance = ln(1 + k·s·t) / k with k = 1.5 / radius, so a shard from the rim flies about 2.1 radii in its life. A shard turns about the axis of one of five spins per copy, with rotational friction 0.2. An explosion lasts 3 s rather than DeepSpaceOutpost's 5, darkens linearly to black, and shrinks each shard to nothing over its last 40%. A structure breaks three times over, as a DeepSpaceOutpost building does, and a ship once. One explosion keeps at most 400 shards, all copies together, chosen at random as DeepSpaceOutpost's fraction does.
5. **`Neuron::MeshPipeline::DrawTriangles` draws geometry made on the CPU each frame.** It copies a triangle list, already in the world, into this frame's slot of an upload buffer and draws it with the pipeline's root signature and state and an identity world matrix. Each frame in flight has one slot of 65,536 vertices, 1.5 MiB. A call that would overflow the slot draws nothing and returns false. The pipeline is opaque and culls back faces, so each shard is drawn twice, once each way round with its normal turned. Each explosion is one draw in its darkened color, newest first, so a frame too busy for every shard drops the oldest.
6. **`GameClient` starts both from the snapshot, before the view takes it.** `DestroyedView` does not say which structure blew up. The client looks the entity up in the newest snapshot it already holds, puts it where the server says it died, and places and colors its model as it drew it. Both play from one tick before the snapshot, as a shot does (ADR-013). The seed is the entity's identifier and the tick, so one destruction always looks the same. The particles have their own `GlowPipeline` and so their own 4,096 instances a frame; past that a frame drops particles, never exhausts. **`CombatEffects` no longer draws an explosion.**
7. **`Outpost::EffectRandom` is the effects' randomness.** It is SplitMix64, like the sky's, and it is presentation, not the simulation's `Random` (ADR-009). Every draw is its own statement, so a seed gives the same draws on every compiler.

## Consequences

- **Q4 gains a draw call for the particles, and one per explosion on screen and its upload.** The heaviest case is the slot full, 65,536 vertices of 24 bytes a frame, and about 27 explosions of 400 shards. That is computed, not measured: the owner's `--measure --stress` run is the measurement, and the stress scene destroys more than 20 ships and structures a minute.
- **The look is unproven.** Every size, speed, color and count is a first guess carried over from DeepSpaceOutpost's units. Only the owner's run says whether a destruction reads from the RTS camera. The constants sit at the top of `ParticleSystem.cpp` and `ExplosionManager.cpp`, as task 3.5's do in `CombatEffects.cpp`.
- **`GameAppTests` checks the math without a GPU.** `ParticleSystemTests` checks timing, seeding, friction, fading, trails, sizing and the cap. `ExplosionManagerTests` checks timing, both faces, drag, darkening and shrinking, placement, seeding, the shard cap, copies, and triangles too small to see. `CombatEffectsTests` checks that a destruction is no longer its job.

## What this forecloses

- Shards that fade with transparency. That would need a blended pipeline with sorting; a shard darkens and shrinks instead.
- Gravity, ground and bouncing particles, which space does not have.
