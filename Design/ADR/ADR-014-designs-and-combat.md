# ADR-014 — Designs are derived stats, hit points count in hundredths, and hits land together at the end of a tick

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-07
Owns: design-stats, starting-designs, hit-points-in-hundredths, weapon-reload, combat-resolution, splash-damage, target-choice

## Context

A design is a hull, a drive and a weapon, and a module where it has one (Game §ships.designs). Hits are instant and weapons are turrets (Game §combat.turrets). `Tools/BattleModel.py` plays the same rules in the abstract, and the balance check holds the simulation against it, so wherever the game's rules leave a detail open the simulation follows the model. ADR-009's replay asks that the order in which a tick adds up its hits never change the result.

## Decision

1. **A design's stats are derived from the tuning data, never stored in it.** `Outpost::DesignStatsFor` combines a hull, a drive, a weapon and, where the design has one, a module ([ADR-058](ADR-058-modules.md)) into hit points, armor, speed, cost, build time, damage, fire interval, range, splash and the module's sight, as the model's `designs_from` does for hull, drive and weapon, through `DesignStatsOf` in `GameProtocol`, which the designer uses too ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md)). A saved design (`ShipDesign`) keeps its stats, so a ship reads them from its design rather than carrying a copy. Research changes them by design, for its player (ADR-017).
2. **Every player starts with the starting designs saved**: one of every combination of the components no research topic unlocks, in hull, drive and weapon order, named as [ADR-069](ADR-069-starting-design-names.md) has them. `InProcessServer` saves them as it adds each player ([ADR-016](ADR-016-base-building.md) decision 3).
3. **Hit points, armor and damage count in hundredths of a point,** as integers (`hitPointsHundredths`, `armorHundredths`, `damageHundredths`), so that the balance check can move armor and damage by 5%, which the tuning data's whole numbers could not hold. A hit does the larger of a quarter of its damage and its damage less the target's armor (`HitHundredths`), the quarter rounded down to a hundredth. A drive's factor and a research upgrade are each rounded once to whole hundredths. Integers make the order of additions irrelevant, so ADR-009's replay holds without reasoning about float sums. The HUD writes hit points as whole points, rounded up, so that a ship with a sliver left does not read as dead.
4. **A weapon reloads in thousandths of a tick.** The fire interval becomes `interval × tick rate × 1000` milliticks, rounded once. Each tick takes 1000 off. The weapon fires on the tick the count reaches zero or less, and the interval is added back, so an interval that is not a whole number of ticks still fires at its average rate.
5. **A cold weapon's first shot comes at a random moment within its interval**, drawn from the simulation's PRNG, as in the model. Volleys of a group then do not land together. A weapon is cold when the ship is new or has had no target for a whole interval past its reload. A ship that loses its target only for a moment, jostled out of range, keeps its rhythm, and a weapon that was ready and waiting fires at once, its next shot a whole interval later. Finding a target never makes a ship fire sooner than its last shot allows.
6. **Shots are chosen from where everything stands at the start of the tick, and land together at its end.** No shot depends on which ship was handled first. As in the model, a target can take more than it needs, so focus fire overkills. Then the destroyed leave the world. Orders on them end, and so do targets.
7. **Range is measured between centers**, as the model measures it between clumps. A big target is not easier to reach.
8. **A ship fires at the nearest enemy ship in its range, and at the nearest enemy structure only when no enemy ship is in range**, and keeps its target until it dies or leaves range. A ship on an attack order fires at its ordered target while that is in range, and otherwise at what that rule picks, so it fires on its way in. Two other rules exist only for the balance check: random and weakest, each chosen again for every shot, the model's spread and focus fire (`Simulation::SetTargetRule`). A match never uses them.
9. **Combat runs before movement in a tick.** An attack-moving ship with a target in range stands still that tick. A ship on an attack order closes along a route the group searched once (ADR-010) at its own top speed, stands while its target is in range, and paths again when the target has moved 40 m from where it last pathed to, at most once a second. Every other ship fires on the move.
10. **What has no hit points is out of combat.** Asteroids, fields and derelicts have none. So do ships and structures placed without them: movement tests and the measurement load (`MeasurementLoad`) place those, and their timings stay comparable. Nothing targets them, and they do not fire.
11. **A snapshot says what happened in its tick**: each shot, with where it went from and to when it was fired, its splash radius and the gun that fired it ([ADR-064](ADR-064-structure-upgrades.md) decision 11), and each entity destroyed, with where it was. The client draws effects from those ([ADR-034](ADR-034-shot-looks.md)). The snapshot also carries hit points for every entity, the player's Ore, and the player's designs with their names; what a player sees of the rest under fog is [ADR-024](ADR-024-fog-of-war.md) decision 6's.
12. **A splash hits every enemy near the target as hard as the target** (owner, 2026-10-01). A shot from a weapon with a `splashRadiusMeters` also hits every other enemy ship and structure whose center is within that radius of the target's center, measured from where everything stood at the start of the tick. Each takes the full hit after its own armor, and the hits land with the others at the end of the tick. There is no falloff with distance. The shooter's own side is never hit, and neither is what has no hit points. The shot in the snapshot carries the radius, so the client can show what the blast reached. The designer's damage cards, and the production window's ratings drawn from them, count every ship of a formation of the hull that one hit reaches (`ShipsReached`): a formation stands its ships three footprint radii apart (ADR-010). A card says how many, "×5 ships" (owner, 2026-10-07).

## Consequences

- **Targeting is O(n²) per tick when targets are lost.** A ship that keeps its target checks only that one. A ship looking for one checks every enemy: at 200 ships, 20,000 distance tests on a tick when everyone looks at once, by count rather than measurement.
- **What a splash reaches depends on the hulls' sizes** (owner, 2026-10-01). A group forms up three footprint radii apart (ADR-010), so a splash can reach the neighbors of a small hull in formation and none of a larger hull's, which it hits only when they are pressed closer while they fight. `Tools/BattleModel.py` gives its clumps that formation spacing so that the model sees the same.
- **Splash costs a pass over the entities per missile shot.** At 200 ships it is 200 distance tests per missile. That is small beside targeting.
- **The client cannot see why a command was rejected.** Attack answers `UnknownTarget` or `NotAnEnemy`, and the protocol has no message that tells the client (ADR-009).

## What this forecloses

- Projectiles with flight time, accuracy, tracking or damage types, without a new ADR.
- Friendly fire.
- Float hit points.
