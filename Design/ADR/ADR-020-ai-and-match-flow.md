# ADR-020 — The AI is a client with its own settings file, a match ends once, and the game opens on a menu

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-08
Owns: ai-player, ai-settings-file, ai-base-plan, ai-counters, ai-territory-play, ai-structure-levels, ai-fleet-cap, ai-any-state, ai-under-fog, match-end, match-end-banner, menu-and-match-screens

## Context

The AI plays a seat no person plays: the opponent in a skirmish, and an AI empire in a world (Game §ai). It must be a client that reads its snapshot and sends commands, and it may include only `GameProtocol` (ADR-002). The owner settled how it plays (owner, 2026-10-01): it researches economy first and then the heavies, answers what it sees with counters held as data, and builds its base by a plan of rigs, Shipyards by income, a Research Lab and Defence Platforms. A match against it has to end once, with a winner or a draw, and the game needs a way into a match and back out of it.

## Decision

1. **The AI is `Outpost::AiPlayer` in `Opponent`.** The shell hands it every snapshot of its player, and it returns the commands to send through its own connection; on a world's server it is hosted there instead ([ADR-079](ADR-079-seat-controller-and-deputy.md)). It watches every snapshot for shots and decides once a second. It is not deterministic across builds, and it need not be: the server logs the commands it applied, so a match replays from its seed and command log without the AI (ADR-009).
2. **The AI's numbers are in `OutpostCommander/Assets/Opponent.json`, not `Tuning.json`.** They are how one client plays, not the rules of the match, and the simulation never reads them. Among them are the attack group's size, `attackGroupShips`, and the review interval; Easy and Hard have files of their own ([ADR-065](ADR-065-ai-difficulty.md) decision 1). `LoadAiSettings` reads the file as strictly as the tuning loader does. It cannot check the identifiers against `Tuning.json`, which only the server reads, so `GameLogicTests` does: every component and topic it names exists, and the research order comes after each topic's prerequisites.
3. **A snapshot shows the hull, drive and weapon of every warship the player sees, whoever owns it** ([ADR-024](ADR-024-fog-of-war.md) decision 6). A design's identifier names a design in its owner's list only, so the components are what the AI counts.
4. **`PlaceGhost` is in `GameProtocol`**, so that the AI places structures by the rule the player's ghost shows. Both apply the server's placement rule to what a snapshot shows (ADR-016).
5. **The base plan is laid at the AI's first decision.** The home asteroids are the `homeAsteroids` nearest its Command Station. The contested ones are the next nearest, leaving out any nearer the enemy's base, as decision 17 places it. The Shipyard, the Research Lab and the home platform stand to the sides and front of the station, toward the map's center. A contested platform stands beside its rig, toward the AI's base. Each place keeps `structureGapMeters` clear of everything else, so that ships still pass, and is found by searching outward in rings when the preferred one is taken. A place is checked again when its structure is ordered. The AI builds the plan in order, at most two Constructors to a site, and the rest go to the next site. A rig whose asteroid another player holds is skipped, and so is its platform. A destroyed structure is built again. Ships are not queued while the next structure waits for Ore. When one of its rigs' asteroids runs dry, it plans a rig, with a platform beside it, on the nearest asteroid it knows still holds ore, outside the enemy's home. On a map with territory, a rig in a sector the AI does not hold follows a Relay on that sector's node, built once the sector is next to the AI's territory (decision 19).
6. **Shipyards join the plan as the income grows.** Once its income reaches N × `incomePerShipyardOrePerSecond` with N − 1 Shipyards planned, the AI plans the N-th: behind the station, then behind it to either side, each round of three farther out, at the nearest clear place. A planned Shipyard waits while the income is below its share, as after a rig is lost. One Shipyard spends its design's cost over its build time, and more once the shipyard topics speed it up, so the packaged share keeps the Shipyards spending about what the rigs earn.
7. **The AI tracks its Constructors itself,** since the server does not report a refused command. It remembers which Constructors it sent where. If the site it ordered has not appeared 3 s later, it takes the order as refused. A site with nobody on it gets the idle Constructors. It sends no Constructor to a structure's next level, which builds itself, and it upgrades a Shipyard below its production design's hull before it queues that design there (decision 14).
8. **Counters, and the AI's fleet.**
   - Every `reviewIntervalSeconds` it finds the most common design, by components, among the enemy warships it has seen since the last review (decision 17), and builds the first of that design's counters it has unlocked, and the default when it has none. `Opponent.json` lists a design's counters in order of preference, the heavy before the triangle's answer. A tie goes to the lowest hull, then drive, then weapon.
   - It saves a design it does not have, and keeps `shipyardQueueJobs` jobs in each Shipyard's queue.
   - Its warships gather in reserve `rallyDistanceMeters` from its Command Station, toward the map's center. On a map with territory they hold a sector instead (decision 13).
   - When the reserve reaches the attack group's size ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 1), they join the attack group. The group goes for the enemy structures it sees or remembers, production first ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 6), its ships close in on its target ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 7), and it falls back once it has lost too many ships ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 2).
   - A shot on any of its structures, built or a site, sends the reserve there with an attack-move (owner, 2026-10-01). The reserve goes back `defenseHoldSeconds` after the last shot. It is sent again only when where it goes, or whether it holds a sector there, changes.
   - The AI never kites.
9. **A match ends once.** A match ends once a player whose base was placed is beaten, by the loss of its production ([ADR-037](ADR-037-losing-all-production.md) decision 1) or by domination ([ADR-057](ADR-057-domination.md) decision 5); a battle matchup ends by [ADR-083](ADR-083-battle-matchups.md) decision 3; and a world never ends ([ADR-084](ADR-084-world-without-an-end.md) decision 2). Every snapshot carries `matchOver`, the winner, and `matchEndedTick`. The winner is no player when both are beaten in the same tick. The simulation runs on after the match ends, and the outcome stands. A simulation with no bases placed never ends, as in the movement and combat tests.
   - **The banner** (`Hud::DescribeOutcome`) says Victory, Defeat or Draw, and under it the match's length, after the words of how it ended where the ending's owner gives them ([ADR-057](ADR-057-domination.md) decision 8, [ADR-083](ADR-083-battle-matchups.md) decision 3). In a world it says that the empire has fallen ([ADR-084](ADR-084-world-without-an-end.md) decision 7).
   - **Its Back to menu is the way from a match to the menu.** A match that has not ended offers none: besides the banner, only a lost connection takes the player back to the menu ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 10). Closing the game leaves the match, and the match log writes that it was left ([ADR-038](ADR-038-phase-one-match-log.md) decision 5).
10. **The game opens on a menu, and `GameClient` has a menu screen and a match screen.** The menu's skirmishes are [ADR-065](ADR-065-ai-difficulty.md) decision 4's, its Join world [ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 9's, and its notice of why the last game ended [ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 10's; Quit ends the game. Starting or leaving a match resets everything the last match left in the view, without reloading a mesh. Each match has its own server and seed.
    - The next match's server is made while the menu shows, and the first one on a thread of its own while the window is still hidden ([ADR-049](ADR-049-startup-in-parallel.md) decisions 2 and 3), so that bad tuning, map or AI data is reported before the screen goes full screen.
    - The AI is player 2, which `Models.json` draws with the Tarkan set.
    - `--measure`, `--load` and `--stress` skip the menu, and under load or stress no AI plays. A measurement run meets the Normal AI.

13. **On a map with territory the AI plays for it.** Its numbers are in `Opponent.json`; on a map without territory none of this happens.
    - **Claims.** Once its first Shipyard stands, it claims the free sector next to its territory nearest its base: a Relay on the node, and a rig on each of the sector's asteroids, which wait for the Relay (decision 5). It claims one sector at a time, once every Relay it has planned stands or waits on the enemy, and at most `claimSectors` beyond those its rigs take it to: a sector its plan already has a Relay for is not a claim.
    - **Its front.** A sector it holds next to one the enemy holds, not its home, is its front. The Relay there gets `frontPlatforms` Defence Platforms beside it, toward the AI's base, once.
    - **A scout.** It keeps `scouts` of `scoutDesign`, a design with the Sensor Array ([ADR-058](ADR-058-modules.md)). A scout is queued ahead of its warships and of a structure waiting for Ore, so that the first sets out in the opening minutes. First it explores its own half, out of the pirates' reach ([ADR-076](ADR-076-ai-plays-phase-4.md) decision 4). Then it attack-moves from one sector next to the enemy's home to the next, so that it meets what is there and suppresses the Relay there while it lives ([ADR-056](ADR-056-territory.md)). The enemy's home is across the map's center from its own. A scout is not a warship of the reserve.
    - **Raids.** While its reserve holds at least twice `raidShips`, that many of it, the smallest hulls, which are the fastest, attack-move on the nearest enemy sector by whose node it sees no enemy warship within 600 m, the enemy's home aside. Those that come within 500 m of the sector's Relay are ordered once to attack it (`CLOSE_IN_METERS`). The raid ends once the sector is no longer the enemy's, or once it has lost `raidLossShare` of its ships. Its ships then rejoin the reserve, and the next raid waits `raidIntervalSeconds`.
    - **Holding.** The reserve holds a sector with a standing order ([ADR-059](ADR-059-alerts-and-standing-orders.md)): its front sector nearest the rally, or the rally's own sector while it has no front. The server then sends it at the enemy ships it sees in the sector, and back to the node.
    - **The main attack** waits for a lead in nodes, or a larger reserve ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 5).
    - **Pirates and derelicts** are [ADR-076](ADR-076-ai-plays-phase-4.md)'s: it clears pirates by a detachment, salvages a derelict with one Constructor at a time, and otherwise leaves both alone.
    - **Ties are mirrored.** Two places as near as each other, such as the two sectors beside its home or beside the enemy's, are told apart by the side of the line from its base to the map's center each lies on, so that both seats of the point-symmetric map choose mirrored places.
14. **The AI plays structure levels** ([ADR-064](ADR-064-structure-upgrades.md)). Its numbers are in `Opponent.json`.
    - **Its Command Station** is upgraded whenever a Relay of its plan waits on the node cap, in that Relay's place in the plan's order, and it goes no higher while it wants no further node. Which of its claims waits for which level follows from the node caps of the station's levels.
    - **Its Shipyards** are upgraded to the level its production design's hull needs, one Shipyard and one level at a time, and the design is queued only at Shipyards of that level: level 2 from the start, since its default design is a Medium hull, and level 3 once it answers with a Large one.
    - **Its Research Lab** is upgraded when the next topic of its research order is of a tier the Lab has not opened, once what the level requires is researched, and to the level that gives a second slot once its open tier is `secondSlotTier` while two topics of its order are left. That level comes after the one that opens tier 3, so any `secondSlotTier` up to 3 takes it at the same moment, and a higher one never takes it.
    - **An upgrade it cannot yet pay for holds back new production**, as a structure waiting for Ore does, so that the Ore gathers for it. A level needs no Constructor, so an upgrade never takes the Ore of a structure of its plan that waits for a Constructor to free up (`AiPlayerTests.BuildsShipyardsByIncome`).
    - **What it attacks.** Within a rank of [ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 6, each level a structure has above the first counts as `attackLevelMeters` nearer the group.
15. **The AI plays the fleet cap** ([ADR-071](ADR-071-fleet-cap.md)). Its Command Station is also upgraded when the cap would hold back a round of its Shipyards, a ship of its production design from each, since a level builds itself for most of a minute; an upgrade it cannot yet pay for holds back production as the others do. Its main attack is sized by the room the cap leaves it ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 1).
16. **The AI starts from any state of its player.** It keeps nothing a server could save, so an AI empire made afresh after its server restarted, or an AI handed an empire it did not build, plays on from the snapshot it is given ([ADR-079](ADR-079-seat-controller-and-deputy.md)).
    - **Its plan adopts what stands.** A slot of a Shipyard, a Research Lab, a Defence Platform or a Repair Bay takes an own structure of its kind that no slot holds, the nearest within 400 m of the slot's preferred place, as far as the search for a free place looks, rather than a free place beside it. A rig's and a Relay's slots stand where their structure does already. The Relays of the sectors it holds beyond its home that no slot holds become slots, and count against `claimSectors`.
    - **Without a Command Station** its plan is laid round its oldest Shipyard, or else its oldest structure. With no structure at all, it waits.
    - **Its player's ships** join its reserve, as its own do between attacks.
    - **In a match nothing is adopted**, since every structure the AI has comes from its plan. A 20-minute AI-against-AI match logs the same commands with and without adoption, by the hash of its command log, over seeds 1 and 2.
    - **Checked** by `AiPlayerTests.PicksUpItsOwnEmpireWhereItLeftOff`: an AI made afresh at minute 12 orders no more base structures in its next minute than the one that never stopped. Also by `TakesOverABaseItDidNotBuild` and `PlaysAnEmpireWithoutAStationFromItsFirstSnapshot`.
17. **The AI plays by what it sees** ([ADR-024](ADR-024-fog-of-war.md)).
    - **Counters.** At each review it counters the most common design among the enemy warships it has seen since the last review, and keeps its design when it has seen none. At the start of a match that is the default design.
    - **Base plan.** It plans its contested asteroids against the enemy's Command Station where it sees one. Until it sees one, it takes the enemy's base to be across the map's center from its own, since the map is point-symmetric.
    - **Attack.** The attack group goes only for an enemy structure it sees or remembers, production first ([ADR-041](ADR-041-ai-plays-a-longer-match.md) decision 6). When it knows none, it goes across the center to look.
    - A rig refused because an enemy rig it cannot see holds the asteroid is handled by decision 7, as any refused site is.
18. **The AI plays on without its Command Station.** It keeps its Shipyards' queues full, researches, builds its plan round where its station stood, and repairs with the Constructors it has. It queues no Constructors.
19. **On a map with territory the AI's plan holds Relays.** Its plan puts a Relay slot on a sector's node before the first rig it plans there. A Relay waits, without holding up the rest of the plan, until its sector is next to one the AI holds, and a rig until its sector is held. A rig or a Relay in a sector the enemy holds or the pirates guard is blocked, as a rig on another's asteroid is. The AI looks for a Shipyard's place only in sectors it holds.

## Consequences

- **The checks behind the AI** are:
  - Scripted snapshots: its first orders, its answer to each design of the triangle, and the reserve going to its Command Station and to a site under fire. For territory: the reserve holding its home and then its front, the main attack waiting for a lead or a larger reserve, a raid on an unguarded sector of the smallest hulls that ends once the sector is free, no raid on a guarded one, and a scout's tour.
  - The real server: its base in order, its Shipyards by income, its answer to an enemy fleet, the attack once its group has gathered, and the defence of an outpost. For territory: a scout in the first three minutes, one claim next to its home and none when the settings ask for none, and a platform by its Relay on the front. For levels: the station upgraded before the claim its node cap holds back, and no higher, the Lab's second slot once tier 3 is open, and a Shipyard's level counted in what it attacks.
  - A whole match against a passive player.
- **Territory's play shortens AI-against-AI matches.** Their lengths are measurements, and the [balance log](../../GameDesign/Records/BalanceLog.md) holds them.

## What this forecloses

- Tuning the AI in `Tuning.json`, or the simulation reading `Opponent.json`.
- The AI reading server state, or acting more often than once a second, without a new decision.
- A match that ends any other way than decision 9's, such as a surrender, without a new decision.
- Snapshots that hide the components of an enemy warship the player sees.
