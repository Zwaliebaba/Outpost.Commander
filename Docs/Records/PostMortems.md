# Outpost Commander — Post-mortems

Each entry is dated the day its phase closed, and new entries are appended in that order.

## 2026-10-01 — MVP: is the game worth building?

The MVP ran from 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-01. Its plan delivered the checkers, six milestones and the tasks the owner inserted, in PRs #26 to #43, with parts of milestones 1 and 2 landed as direct commits, and it closed on 2026-10-01, when all five of its questions had an answer.

**The question.** Is this game worth building? The MVP admitted nothing that did not help answer it, and built the least of a Warzone 2100-style strategy game in space that could: one 2,000 m map with two starts, 6 home and 6 contested ore asteroids and 9 asteroid fields; five structures; designs of one hull, one drive and one weapon, from three hulls, two drives and three weapons, 18 designs in all; eight research topics; and one scripted AI, The Tarkan High Command, playing through the same commands as the player. It split the question into five. A failed answer was still a result, and the MVP was done when all five were answered, not when all five were "yes".

- **Q1, the loop.** Is mine, build, research, design, fight fun? A full match against the AI is one the owner wants to play again, and lasts 15–25 minutes.
- **Q2, design.** Does ship design matter? The Q2 check passes. Designs fight in clumps bought with equal Ore, under spread fire (every ship shoots a random enemy) and focus fire (every ship shoots the weakest), at 2,000, 3,000, 4,500, 6,000, 9,000 and 12,000 Ore a side with every component, and at 2,000–4,500 Ore with only the starting components. It passes when (a) every design has a counter that beats it in at least four battles of five; (b) the designs worth building, those the equilibrium mix of the win-rate matrix gives 5% or more, use every hull, drive and weapon of the stage; (c) none of those counters stops winning when any single number moves 5%; and (d) at the starting budgets, no research topic taken by one side alone gives it a design that none of the other side's starting designs beats in half its battles. A threshold counts as met only when the 95% confidence interval of the win rate clears it.
- **Q3, pacing.** Does research drive the pacing? Research choices visibly change what is built in the mid-game.
- **Q4, the engine.** With 200 ships and 40 structures in combat at 1920×1080 and the HUD drawn, 99% of frames take at most 16.7 ms and a tick takes at most 5 ms.
- **Q5, the boundary.** The client and the AI can include only the protocol headers, so reaching into server state does not compile, and an order shows on screen within 150 ms: ADR-002's 50–100 ms for the tick and the interpolation, plus about two frames for input and presentation.

**What it found.** All five were answered on 2026-10-01.

- **Q1 was "yes"** (owner), from the owner's first two matches against the AI, summarized by `Tools/MatchLog.py`. In the first, seed 17908775201362716, the owner left at 24:51, finding the AI too tough: it had built 443 warships to the player's 141, and 26 to 1 in the first five minutes. In the second, seed 17908808925176743, the owner won at 14:31 with 55 warships to the AI's 261, just under Q1's 15 minutes. The owner decided that the AI can be beaten as it is, and it was not changed for the MVP.
- **Q2 was "no" at milestone 3, and "yes" after a retune against the simulation.** The check passed in `Tools/BattleModel.py` and failed when milestone 3 played it as headless battles in the real simulation, and milestones 4 to 6 were built on those numbers. Once the Missile Rack was in, the simulation failed all four criteria: the Missile Rack, taken by one side with Mass Driver Calibration, left the other side's starting designs without a single win at 3,000–4,500 Ore. The owner decided on 2026-10-01 that the simulation is the game, so balance is tuned against its check and the model stays a rough guide that is not taught the simulation's geometry; and that (b) is judged over each stage's budgets rather than at every one, since no candidate made a heavy hull worth building in a 2,000 Ore fight and a Medium one in a 12,000 Ore fight at once. About twenty candidates were run through the simulation's check, and four numbers moved: the Lance's fire interval from 3.0 s to 2.7 s, Mass Driver Calibration's fire rate from +15% to +10%, the Missile Rack's hit from 40 to 30 and its cost from 110 to 130 Ore. All four criteria then passed. Every design's best counter won at least 80% at every budget, nearly all of them 100%; (b) held over each stage's budgets in both modes; none of 2,226 counters flipped under a 5% change; and the weakest answer to a one-sided topic was the brawler against the plated swarm, at 55% with Hull Plating and 59% with Mass Driver Calibration, while Lance Focusing left an answer at 87%. That run took 278 s on four cores in the Linux container. The check of record ran on the development machine in Release|ARM64 on 2026-10-01, in 1 min 51 s on ten cores, with the same verdicts, but made 2,304 counter checks where the container made 2,226: the two builds do not play every battle alike, which ADR-009's promise of a replay on the same build and platform allows.
- **The model and the simulation disagreed, and the simulation decided.** `Tools/BattleModel.py`, 80 s, passed (a) only: no design worth building used the Missile Rack; the swarm stopped beating the line under spread fire when the Mass Driver's damage dropped 5%; and one side's Lance Focusing left its line without an answer above 13–25%. The model puts every ship of a clump in range of every enemy, so it values the Lance's reach more and the Missile Rack's splash less than the simulation does, where a ship reaches only the enemies near it. A review of the model on 2026-09-30 had already found its win rates measuring where each budget cut a design's ship count, and 60 unstratified battles unable to tell 53% from 48%; it was changed then to spread its budgets evenly, field the leftover Ore as a fractional ship and take its verdicts on 95% intervals.
- **The check cannot price a drive.** In a battle between two clumps that close and fire, speed decides only who fires first. What speed is worth, reaching a raid, leaving a losing fight or crossing the map, was left to play, and Q2's (b) was not tightened for drives.
- **Fog of war changed no battle.** With ADR-024's fog on, on 2026-10-02, the full check gave a report byte-identical to the one without it, its 2,226 counter checks included, since every armed ship sees beyond its weapon's range. It was measured in the Linux container by running the check with and without its fog option.
- **Q3 was "yes"** (owner). In the first match the AI moved to Large+Fusion designs after its Large Hull at 6:42, and its first heavies came between 10:00 and 15:00. The player had researched all eight topics by 13:04 and built Small and Medium hulls with the Mass Driver and the Lance, never the Large hull, the Fusion Drive or the Missile Rack, partly because Queue then worked only on a design already saved (ADR-023).
- **Q4 was met for frames at 1920×1080, and for ticks in steady play. It was missed on the ticks that order 200 ships at once, and on a few ticks in combat.** Every figure, on both machines, with how each was measured, is in [BalanceLog.md](BalanceLog.md) (2026-09-30 and 2026-10-01).

  The slow ticks were the orders: pathing and forming up two groups of 100 ships in one tick. On the second machine on 2026-09-30, with both fleets kept moving over 1,181 ticks, a tick took 0.18 ms on average and 0.37 ms at the 99th percentile, and the order ticks took 3.0, 3.7, 2.7, 5.0, 6.8 and 8.3 ms, missing on 2 of 6. The owner decided on 2026-10-01 that a group's order paths once for the group rather than once per ship (ADR-010), which cut the same order tick from about 3.4 ms to about 1 ms in a Linux container, and to record Q4 with the slow ticks and fix them after the MVP. On the development machine the order ticks still took 5.7–8.7 ms, by then with the load's structures blocking movement.
- **The Adreno's GPU timestamps missed work.** At 2880×1920 the logged work was about 3 ms of CPU and 3 ms of GPU a frame, yet every frame took two vsyncs: something outside the two timestamps cost more than 16.7 ms. On that machine the frames presented, not the GPU work, say whether a frame was on time. The same view at 1920×1080 held 60 frames a second, as did every view at 2880×1920 with less of the fight on screen. Why the timestamps miss the work was not found.
- **Q5 was met.** On the development machine, from an injected right-click to the first presented frame in which an ordered ship visibly responds, over 30 move orders to the two Constructors at rest: mean 48 ms, median 49, 95th percentile 66, worst 70; from when the game read the click, mean 38 and worst 55. On the second machine on 2026-09-30: mean 43 ms, median 44, 95th percentile 66, worst 67; from the read, mean 38 and worst 62. `#include "GameLogic.h"` added to `GameApp` and to `Opponent` failed to compile in both with C1083, as it should.
- **The base's intents held, two of them narrowly.** The hand checks (`HandCheckTests`) ran in the Linux container, and the simulation is deterministic, so their figures are the game's. With the raid gathering 400 m off, out of the gun's reach: a lone Defence Platform destroyed five Small+Ion+Mass Driver ships in 41.7 s and kept 420 of its 1,500 hit points, 28%; the armed Command Station destroyed seven in 57.6 s, 2.4 s inside its minute, keeping 2,952 of 5,000; and a Command Station without its gun or armor fell to seven in 24.9 s, which is why it is armed.
- **The AI needed its economy sized by its income.** With a second Shipyard at 30 Ore a second it earned three times what its two Shipyards spent. With a Shipyard for each 10 Ore a second of income (owner, 2026-10-01) it had 4 by 2:15 and beat a player who did nothing at 5:24. It researched the Fusion Drive and the Large Hull without ever building a heavy, until it was told to answer the swarm and the brawler with heavies once it had them. Two AIs on the real server ended a match in 8 to 15 minutes, with heavies from 6:35.
- **Two working rules came of it, and both hold.** Where the model and the simulation disagree, the simulation is right. A slow suite is gated by an environment variable, since the native test adapter ignores vstest's category filter: CI's first try at filtering the full check out ran it in Debug until the job timed out.

**How it was measured.** The development machine is the owner's Snapdragon X laptop of ADR-003, running the Release|ARM64 build, and the second machine an x64 laptop running Release|x64; scripts drove both. [BalanceLog.md](BalanceLog.md) describes the machines, and gives each figure with its method.

**What the next phase took on.** The owner's next question, answered on 2026-10-02, was whether a match can last long enough to be a campaign of its own and still be the same game. A match was over in about five minutes when the owner rushed the AI and in 15–25 otherwise, and the owner wanted it to last up to two hours. Slowing every clock by five would have given the MVP's fifteen minutes in slow motion, and the MVP runs out of things to do at about minute 13: its eight topics take 11½ minutes of research, and it has 18 designs. So Phase 1 was to make a match longer by giving it stages: tiers of research that change what is built, a map that has to be taken outward as its ore runs out, and a win condition that one raid cannot settle. Its first target was 45–60 minutes, and two hours was to be decided from what that measured. It was also to fix the order ticks that missed Q4, against Q4's own target, no tick over 5 ms with 200 ships and 40 structures, orders included, on the development machine in Release|ARM64, and to start with a measurement of where an order tick's time goes rather than a fix. Beside those it took on the owner's mockup of the designer, windows that float over the battlefield, and ships that bank in their turns.

Between the MVP's close and Phase 1's start the owner had more built, from 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-03:

- the sky as an even field of stars, with no galaxy, band or nebula (ADR-022);
- Queue in the designer saving a new design and queuing it in one click (ADR-023);
- fog of war, the first feature after the MVP, under which the AI counters only what it has seen (ADR-024);
- the server's ticks on a thread of their own, so that a slow tick no longer holds up a frame (ADR-025);
- explosions and particles after DeepSpaceOutpost, low-poly models that show their ridges as lit lines, and a pixel-wide grid with the brightest stars as crosses (ADR-026 to ADR-028);
- on 2026-10-03, dark faces under multisampled lines, with structures drawn behind the ships (ADR-040).

The owner's own low-poly models replaced the placeholder meshes on 2026-10-02.

**ADRs.** ADR-001 to ADR-020 and ADR-022 came of the MVP itself, and ADR-023 to ADR-028 and ADR-040 of the work just after it. The number between ADR-020 and ADR-022 is a gap. Until the MVP was done its ADRs were edited in place, and its plan froze them when it closed.

**Left undone or deferred, and where it went.**

- **The order ticks over 5 ms** went to Phase 1, which measured them and fixed them (ADR-032).
- **The heavy view at 2880×1920 at 30 frames a second, and the Adreno's timestamps that miss work,** were recorded, outside Q4's resolution, and no later phase took them up.
- **The thin Defence margins**, where a sixth raider or one more point of Mass Driver damage may tip the platform and the station's minute is 2.4 s from failing, were left for the owner's playtests (owner, 2026-10-01). No later document records a verdict on them. Phase 4 later tripled the Defence Platform's cost with the ships' and left its gun as it was.
- **The model** stayed a rough guide that agrees with the simulation on (a) only.
- **The out-of-scope list** was a campaign; multiplayer over a network; save and load; fog of war; other races; carriers, fighters and VTOL-style rearming; commanders; sensors and artillery; transports and haulers; asteroid depletion; Huge and VeryLarge hulls; structure upgrades; damage types; unit veterancy; textures and materials; music; options, key rebinding and a real menu; and AI difficulty levels. Later work brought in fog of war the next day (ADR-024), depletion in Phase 1 (ADR-035), a sensor module in Phase 2 (ADR-058), structure upgrades and difficulty levels in Phase 3 (ADR-064, ADR-065), the match's players reaching the in-process server over QUIC beside Phase 2's close (ADR-060), and save and load for a world, on a dedicated server, in Phase 5 (ADR-077, ADR-078). Each later phase kept the rest out, and Phase 4's own list named veterancy, artillery and spotting, and new hulls and weapons among them.
- **The two tasks added to the plan after it closed**, the explosions and the dark faces, were built, as ADR-026 to ADR-028 and ADR-040 record and the code has them, but the closed plan's board still listed both as in review and not yet built or run.

## 2026-10-03 — Phase 1: a longer match, made of stages

Phase 1 ran from 2026-10-02, when the owner answered its questions and decided most of its design's gates, to 2026-10-03, when it closed with P1 to P5 recorded. Its milestones 7 to 13 landed in PRs #49, #51 and #52, and the owner's startup and frame performance work landed beside them in #53.

**The question.** Can a match last long enough to be a campaign of its own, and still be the same game? Phase 1 made a match longer by giving it stages rather than by slowing it, and aimed at 45–60 minutes first (owner, 2026-10-02). It added three research tiers: the MVP's eight topics as tier 1, and 17 more behind two gateway topics, 2,590 s of research, about 43 minutes with one Lab. It added the Pulse Drive, the Flak Battery and the Rail Cannon, 45 designs where the MVP had 18. It replaced the map with one 5 km a side, with four rings of ore, 24 asteroids holding 285,000 Ore, whose reserves run out to a trickle of 20% of their yield. And a player now lost only with neither a Command Station nor a finished Shipyard. Beside the stages it brought the designer to the owner's mockup as a window of its own, made the screens float, banked ships in their turns and fixed the MVP's slow order ticks. Its five questions:

- **P1, the length.** A match the owner plays against the AI without rushing lasts 45–60 minutes, and the median of 10 seeded AI-against-AI matches is recorded with its spread.
- **P2, the tiers.** What each side builds after it finishes a tier's gateway differs visibly from what it built before.
- **P3, design.** The balance check, restructured per tier, passes all four criteria at every tier.
- **P4, the engine.** The order ticks meet 5 ms at 200 ships, and frame and tick times are recorded on the 5 km map at the ship counts a 60-minute match reaches.
- **P5, the look.** The owner judges the designer against the mockup, the floating windows and the banking, in play.

The owner accepted three risks against the length target (2026-10-02). The AI was not changed to resist a rush, so a rush on its only Shipyard, beside its Command Station, still ended a match in minutes, and P1 was judged on matches the owner did not rush and on AI-against-AI matches. There was no unit cap, though the AI had built 443 warships in the MVP's first match, so a long match on a larger map would go past anything measured. And there was no save, load or pause, so a match was played in one sitting.

**What it found** (owner, 2026-10-03). P5 was "yes". P1 to P4 were recorded as the Linux container measured them, and what only the owner's play or the development machine could add was not measured.

- **P1 was answered for two AIs, not for the owner.** The first run of the switch that plays AI-against-AI matches headlessly (ADR-038) found two AIs ending a match at a median of 22 minutes over seeds 1–40, from 19:37 to 32:18, none within 45–60; they opened tier 2 at 15:04 and never reached tier 3. With ships turning in arcs the median was 23:32 over 140 seeds, and a match was decided by one battle at about minute 12, before tier 2 could matter. The AI was then changed, through its settings file where it could be, to play a longer match (ADR-041): an attack group of 20 warships and 12 more for each tier it has opened, where the MVP's was 12; a fall-back to its rally once an attack has lost 30% of its ships, then a wait before the next; and 2 Defence Platforms round its base for each Shipyard. With a wait of 150 s that gave a median of 53.9 minutes over seeds 1–40, its middle half 43.0–74.0; 50.8 over seeds 41–80, of which 3 had not ended at 150 minutes; and 56.3 over seeds 1–10. Of all 80, 23 ended within 45–60 minutes. Tried and dropped: falling back from a stronger fleet, which gave 32–34 minutes, and leaving the enemy's base alone until a later tier, which gave 26 minutes at tier 2 and stalled 36 of 40 matches at tier 3. Groups that pass an obstacle side by side (ADR-047) then lengthened the matches to 66.4 and 60.5 minutes, so the wait was cut to 120 s, and the figure of record became a median of 47.5 minutes over seeds 1–40 and 51.1 over seeds 41–80. The owner's own matches against the AI were not measured.
- **P2 was not answered.** 78 of 80 AI-against-AI matches reached tier 2, and only 2 reached tier 3: an AI at war seldom affords the 600-Ore gateway. Whether what each side builds changes after a gateway was not checked in the match log.
- **P3 was "yes" in the container.** At their first numbers, run at 20 battles a pairing without (c), the new tiers failed. The Flak Battery beat every Small design 20–0 and lost 0–20 to every Medium and Large one, so it was never worth building, and its 20 m splash reached no neighbor of a formation that stands Small hulls 24 m apart, three footprint radii (ADR-010); a 26 m splash, or a hit of 12 or 14, still lost every battle to a Medium design. The Mass Driver was never worth building at tier 3, nor under focus fire at tier 2, which the MVP's numbers already gave from 4,500 Ore up. Ablative Armour at +15% left the plated Medium Lance line without an answer better than 37% at 6,000 Ore under focus fire. The owner decided that (b) at tiers 2 and 3 asks only the components each tier adds to be worth building, and that the Flak Battery gets a role against the Medium hull as well as against the swarm (2026-10-03). The tuning took the Flak Battery from 9 to 20 a hit, from 140 to 200 m, from a 20 to a 26 m splash and from 70 to 60 Ore, and Ablative Armour from +15% to +5%: a Medium+Ion+Lance line with every plating of tiers 1 and 2 holds 585 hit points, 6.7 of the picket's 87-point Lance hits, anything above about 4% more takes it to eight, and then the picket, its only answer among the tier 2 designs, won under half its battles at 6,000 Ore: 33% under focus fire at +15%, and 45% at +10%. At +5% it still breaks in seven hits, and the picket won 95–100% at every tier 3 budget. The Pulse Drive and the Rail Cannon kept their first numbers. The check of record then passed all four criteria at every stage: 60 battles a pairing, up to 480 when uncertain, and 3,604 counter checks under one-number changes of ±5%, in 69 minutes on four threads. The Flak Battery was worth building at every tier 2 budget, in Small, Medium and Large designs, and the Rail Cannon at tier 3. No design worth building used the Pulse Drive, which (b) does not ask of it, since the check cannot price speed. The weakest answer to a researched design was 93%, the picket against the Medium Lance line with Composite Plating, and 97% with Ablative Armour as well. The Rail Cannon had been meant to answer the Lance picket by outranging it by 20 m, and in the simulation it does not, since ships stand at their own range rather than kite; so the Rail Cannon is the weapon against the heavy, and the Flak Battery the answer to the picket (owner, 2026-10-03). The run of record in Release|ARM64 on the development machine was not made.
- **P4 was not answered.** The measurement (ADR-032) was taken in a Linux build of the simulation with clang 18, which runs the MVP's order ticks in about a third of the development machine's time, timed by the tick's parts and counted with callgrind on the MVP's 200-ship load. The 20.7 ms first order tick was the four path graphs, which match setup dropped by placing the bases after building them. The later order ticks were the ships' paths, about 60 full searches each, mostly line tests against every obstacle. Parting ships cost 0.07–0.2 ms a tick. The fix builds the graphs after setup and on quiet ticks, plans an order of more than 32 ships over two ticks, and makes a search cheaper for the same answer. Taking each tick's least time over five runs, the worst order tick fell from 2.58 ms to 1.24 ms and the first from 6.5–10 ms to 0.5–1.0 ms; by the ratio, that is about 4.2 ms on the development machine against 8.7. A large group sets off a tick later, at most 50 ms, inside Q5's 150 ms. The development machine's `--measure --load`, and frame and tick times on the 5 km map at a 60-minute match's peak ship count, were not measured.
- **P5 was "yes"** (owner, 2026-10-03): the designer, the floating windows and the banking read in play. The owner accepted every task as built.
- **Turning in arcs changed little but the look.** The owner judged it unnatural that a ship stopped at a sharp corner, pivoted on the spot and went on, and had ships fly the way they face and turn as they go, slowing to half speed in a sharp turn (ADR-039). In the container, a Large+Fusion ship sent 100 m behind itself arrived in 8.55 s against 7.90 s, and a point 15 m abeam, which the old rule circled for seconds and gave up 2 m short of, was reached exactly in 0.75–3.6 s, by hull. The balance check run again with arcs passed (a) to (c) at every stage, and (d) was unsure at tier 1: the brawler answered the plated swarm in 53% of 480 battles at 2,000 Ore under spread fire, where it had won 55%. The later check of record passed it. With the arcs, the owner raised the bank limits to 50°, 45° and 35° for the Small, Medium and Large hulls and 30° for the Constructor, and had every ship reach its limit in a sharp turn, Fusion ships included.
- **A seat bias followed the movement rule.** Player 1 won 30 of 40 matches with the same AI in both seats, and the order in which the server applies the players' commands was ruled out as the cause. With arcs, player 2 won 89 of 140. After ADR-041's changes the seats won 39 and 38 of 80. The cause was not found.
- **What the tests caught.** `AiPlayerTests.ReachesTierThree`, in which the AI opens tier 3 at tick 37,200, 31 minutes into a match against a player who does nothing, is the longest test in CI, about four minutes in Debug, and stays there (owner, 2026-10-03): it caught depletion starving the AI's research, its income falling to the trickle at minute 21 before it learned to build on the nearest asteroids with ore left. On the 5 km map the AI's attack-move left its fleet in formation out of its guns' reach of the Command Station it had come for; once its ships within 500 m of their target structure were ordered to attack it, it beat a passive player at 7.8 minutes, where it had taken 5.4 on the 2 km map.

**What the next phase took on.** The owner's question of 2026-10-03: a match was two players building ships and, in the end, one siege of the enemy's base, with no real skirmishes along the way. The owner wanted the fights of Warzone 2100, a scout meeting a scout in the first five minutes and a small enemy outpost a target halfway through, and beyond that a much bigger world with fights over big areas of space, as in PlanetSide. Phase 2 found that the rules made a siege the right play:

- **Nothing in between was worth fighting for.** The home asteroids stood 260 m from the Command Station, under one Defence Platform's cover. The rings paid 5 Ore a second at home, 6 near, 8 contested and 10 rich, and depletion only began to push a player outward.
- **A small fight was a bad trade.** Battles between clumps follow Lanchester's square law, so concentration wins. The armed Command Station beat seven raiders and a Defence Platform five, and a raid of three ships fed the defender.
- **Winning a small fight earned nothing back.** A destroyed rig cost its owner 50 Ore.
- **Seeing the enemy meant fighting it.** Sight was a ship's weapon range and 50 m, so a Small Mass Driver ship saw 170 m.
- **The AI massed before it moved**, with an attack group of 20 warships and 12 more for each tier, and a 120 s regroup after a fall-back.

What makes PlanetSide work is players, not area: there, thousands each run one soldier. Here one commander runs an army, and a bigger map alone gives long travel, losses nobody saw, and still one decisive battle at the end. So Phase 2 was to make territory the thing fought over, a lattice that keeps several fights going along a front, and the tools to command it, on the 5 km map that Phase 1 had laid out in nine sectors with a node site each for that purpose (ADR-036). It kept the 45–60 minute target; two hours was "not yet" (owner, 2026-10-03). It also took Phase 1's one unbuilt task: a group given an Attack order on one enemy keeps its lanes round an obstacle, as a group given a move or an attack-move does.

**ADRs.** ADR-029 to ADR-039 and ADR-041 to ADR-047 came of Phase 1: banking (ADR-029), typography and sprites (ADR-030), floating windows (ADR-031), the order ticks (ADR-032), research tiers (ADR-033), shot looks (ADR-034), ore depletion (ADR-035), map sectors (ADR-036), losing all production (ADR-037), the match log and the AI-against-AI switch (ADR-038), turning in arcs (ADR-039), the AI's longer match (ADR-041), and the owner's three looks at the screen (ADR-042 to ADR-044, ADR-046 and ADR-047). ADR-045, the owner's models with five levels and spinning parts, came beside the plan, and the owner's startup and frame performance work gave ADR-048 to ADR-055. During Phase 1 an accepted ADR was not edited: a changed decision was a new ADR that superseded part of an older one, as ADR-030, ADR-037, ADR-039 and ADR-041 did, and ADR-040 for the MVP's look. From Phase 2 on, ADRs were edited in place again.

**Left undone or deferred, and where it went.**

- **An attacking group's lanes** went to Phase 2, which built them (ADR-047).
- **The owner's own matches for P1, and P2's check of what is built after a gateway,** were not made. Phase 3 asked whether tier 3 is reached as part of its T3, by which time the Research Lab's levels had replaced the gateways (ADR-033).
- **P3's run of record on the development machine, and P4's runs there** (`--measure --load`, and the 5 km map at a 60-minute match's peak ship count), were not made. Phase 4's U7 asked the engine's question again, on its 10 km map, as an owner's run.
- **Two balance questions were left to the owner, and no decision is recorded on either.** One is whether the shares of what is worth building may move with large orders planned over two ticks. ADR-032 names the two that moved more than a few points: the Medium hull, as M+I+La at 21%, became worth building at 12,000 Ore under spread fire, and the starting stage at 4,500 Ore under spread fire became an even three-way of S+I+MD, M+I+MD and M+I+La. The other is the arcs' unsure (d) at tier 1, where nothing was retuned until the owner decided.
- **Two hours** stayed "not yet". Phase 2 kept 45–60 minutes, and Phase 4 set no length target at all, since the horizon makes the game a server game.
- **A locked card in the designer** takes no click, so it is not under the pointer as a button is, and hovering it previews nothing.
- **The seat bias** stayed unexplained. Phase 3 found player 1 winning 28 of 40 again, and Phase 4's placement from the seed had each player winning 20.

## 2026-10-04 — Phase 2: territory worth fighting over

Phase 2 ran from 2026-10-03, when the owner accepted its design and decided all seven of its gates, to 2026-10-04, when its milestones 14 to 19 were merged, in PRs #55 to #60, and the owner ran it and answered S5.

**The question.** Are there skirmishes before the decisive battle once territory is the thing fought over, and can one commander follow them? On Phase 1's 5 km map of nine sectors, a Constructor claims a sector's node by building a Relay on it, adjacent to a sector its player holds, and the home sector is held by the Command Station from the first tick and is never suppressed. Ore belongs to sectors, so a Mining Rig stands and earns only in a sector its player holds, the flanks included. An enemy warship within 400 m of a Relay, with none of its owner's, suppresses it while it stays, which stops the sector's income and the Relay's sight but not its holding. A sector cut off from home through the lattice earns half. Beside losing all production, a match ends by domination: each player starts with 1,000 tickets, and every 10 s the player holding fewer nodes loses 30 times the difference divided by the map's nodes, so a lead of one node of nine drains a side in 50 minutes. A design gained an optional module slot, whose first module, the Sensor Array, lets a ship see 700 m whatever its weapon, and the player gained alerts and two standing orders, hold a sector and patrol. Each question that rests on the owner's play also had an AI-against-AI figure, so that it had an answer whether or not the owner measured it (owner, 2026-10-03):

- **S1, contact.** The first shot is fired by minute 5, in the owner's match and in at least 8 of 10 seeded AI-against-AI matches.
- **S2, skirmishes.** Before minute 20 there are at least five engagements, ships of both sides firing within one sector, in at least three sectors.
- **S3, territory decides.** Over the same 10 matches, both endings happen, domination and losing all production.
- **S4, the length.** The median of the 10 is 45–60 minutes.
- **S5, one commander.** The owner judges the alerts and the standing orders in play.

**What it found** (2026-10-04). - **S2 was met by two AIs:** before minute 20, seeds 1–10 had a median of 16.5 engagements in 6 sectors, and every one of seeds 1–40 had at least five in three sectors.

The AI read "the enemy's front has thinned" as a reserve half as large again as its attack group, since it sees only part of the enemy's front.

**What the next phase took on.** On 2026-10-03 the owner had given the Command Station, the Shipyard and the Research Lab five levels each, in both mesh sets, "to be used later as the structures grow" (ADR-045). The owner's direction of 2026-10-04 was that a Shipyard is upgraded before it builds bigger ships, a Command Station to control more sectors, and a Research Lab before it researches further. Phase 2 had given the map something to fight over between the bases, and Phase 3 was to give the base the same. A Shipyard was 300 Ore wherever it stood and however long it had stood, so losing one cost little more than the time to rebuild it; a level 3 Shipyard that is the only place a Large hull comes from is a target worth a raid, and worth a fleet to defend. Upgrades also add a choice to every minute of a match, since Ore spent on a level is Ore not spent on ships or Relays. The risks were to what Phase 2 had shown: a hard cap on the nodes a player holds is a second brake on territory beside the lattice and the Relay's cost; domination decides only while the two sides hold different numbers of nodes; and gating hulls and tiers behind upgrades could stretch a match past 45–60 minutes or push tier 3 further out of reach than Phase 1's 2 matches in 80. ## 2026-10-04 — Phase 3: a base that grows

Phase 3 ran on 2026-10-04, from the owner's answers and gate decisions of that day to its close the same day. Its milestones 20 to 25, and the owner's request for difficulty levels, landed in PRs #67 to #73, and #74 closed it.

**The question.** Does a base that grows add decisions and targets, without undoing what Phase 2 showed? A structure is upgraded one level at a time. It builds the level itself over its time, with no Constructor, keeps working meanwhile, gains 20% of its kind's base hit points a level and never armor, and loses its levels when it is destroyed. A Shipyard builds Small hulls at level 1, Medium at level 2 and Large at level 3, and the Large Hull topic stays. The Research Lab's levels 2 and 3 open research tiers in place of the two gateway topics, at their Ore and prerequisites, and its level 4 runs a second research slot. The Command Station's level caps the nodes a player holds, from 3 to 7, and gives it a second Defence gun at level 3 and a third at level 5. Every level is drawn within level 1's footprint, so no upgrade changes placement. Its questions, over seeds 1–40 for each one the AIs can answer:

- **T1, Phase 2 holds.** S1 to S4 are met again.
- **T2, the cap's stall.** The time during which both players are at their cap and hold equal numbers of nodes, which nothing drains, has a median under 5 minutes a match.
- **T3, the levels reached.** In at least half of the matches each side reaches level 3 of the Command Station, of a Shipyard and of the Research Lab, and tier 3 is opened in at least half, against Phase 1's 2 in 80.
- **T4, upgraded structures fought over.** In the median match at least one structure above level 1 is attacked.
- **T5, it reads.** The owner judges whether a structure's level shows at a glance, and whether what the next level gives is clear before buying it.

**What it found** (2026-10-04). The AI-against-AI figures were measured in the Linux container, with ADR-038's match log and the AI of ADR-020 and ADR-041 as the phase's last task tuned it, and MSVC's build may play the same seeds differently (ADR-009).

Its question: with fewer, dearer ships on a map worth exploring, is the opening spent finding things out, and does a ship become worth keeping? From Phase 3 it took the node caps, which Phase 3 had left to the phase that builds a larger map to scale, and the Command Station's level as the pattern for a bound on fleets. ## 2026-10-08 — Phase 4: fewer ships, a map worth exploring

**The question.** With fewer, dearer ships on a map worth exploring, is the opening spent finding things out, and does a ship become worth keeping? A Defence Platform cost three times as much too, since a platform is a ship that cannot move: left at 150 Ore while the ships tripled, it would have traded against a fleet at a third of its old rate, and both sides would have turtled. A fleet cap counted command points, Small 1, Medium 2 and Large 4, against a cap set by the Command Station's level, 12, 20, 30, 40 and 50 at levels 1 to 5: tied to the station rather than to the nodes held, so that a lead in nodes is not also a lead in fleet on top of domination.
