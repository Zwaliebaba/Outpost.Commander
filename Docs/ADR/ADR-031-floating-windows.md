# ADR-031 — Windows float over the HUD in layers, their state in a manager and their geometry in the layout

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-08
Owns: floating-windows, window-state, window-placement, window-layers, window-opening

## Context

The game's screens float as windows rather than sit in one place: the designer, the research and a structure's production queue float, and the Ore, the minimap, the selection panel and the command card stay anchored. The structures the Constructors build are the command card's build mode rather than a window, since a window of them would outlive the Constructor that builds them ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 2). A window is dragged by its title bar and kept on the screen, comes to the front when clicked, closes with its × or with Esc, and keeps its place; the match runs on while one is open (owner, 2026-10-02). A place the player leaves a window in is kept across sessions too (owner, 2026-10-27), which [ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md) owns. The HUD is laid out afresh every frame by `Hud::Lay`, which keeps no state and draws nothing (ADR-015): it lists panels, lines, sprites, texts and buttons in pixels. Input focus is a rectangle test on that list.

## Decision

1. **`Outpost::WindowManager` holds the state, and nothing else.** Which windows are open and in what order front to back, where each was left, which is being dragged and where it was taken hold of, all in the HUD's reference units (ADR-006), on the engine's window stack ([ADR-100](ADR-100-the-canvas.md) decision 4); and, the game's own, which of the two places under the Ore an unmoved production or research window took as it opened. It knows no geometry, so it is tested on its own. `GameClient` owns one for as long as the game runs; leaving a match closes every window but keeps their places. The places the player leaves are kept from one session to the next by [ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md), which puts them back with `WindowManager::Place`.
2. **`Hud::Lay` lays each open window out, back to front, after everything else.** It takes the manager, and places each window where the manager left it, or at the first place its row of the windows' table names ([ADR-100](ADR-100-the-canvas.md) decision 8). For production and research that is the first of the two places under the column's kept place ([ADR-066](ADR-066-production-status-on-the-hud.md) decision 3) that no open window held when it opened. A window is moved only as far as `Hud::KeepOnScreen` needs to keep its whole title bar, and 120 units of its width, on the screen; one that stands at its first place's anchor stands on the screen while it fits, and where it does not, at the screen's margin (ADR-100 decision 2). The clamp is applied when the window is laid out, so a smaller screen moves it back too, and `GameClient` settles the manager on the clamped place, so that a window dragged past an edge moves again as soon as the pointer comes back. Without a manager, `Lay` shows every window the content has at its first place, which is what the HUD's tests rely on.
3. **A window is a frame, a hatched title bar, a close box and four corner brackets** (ADR-030), opened by the canvas's `BeginWindow` ([ADR-100](ADR-100-the-canvas.md) decisions 6 and 7), which begins a layer of the display list for it ([ADR-100](ADR-100-the-canvas.md) decision 1) and records where its buttons start among the layout's actions.
4. **The layout is drawn and clicked in layers,** the display list's ([ADR-100](ADR-100-the-canvas.md) decision 1). Layer 0 is the HUD and layer i + 1 is the i-th window back to front; each layer's panels, then its lines, then its sprites, then its texts, so that a window covers the text of the window behind it. The bars over the entities come before every layer ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 5). `LayerAt` finds the front layer under a point, and `ActionAt` takes a press only on a region of that layer, whose payload names the button's action, so a HUD button under a window takes no click. A press on any window is the HUD's, not the world's ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 5).
5. **Input.** A press on a window brings it to the front; on its close box it closes it, and on its title bar it starts a drag that follows the pointer until the button is released. Esc closes the front window and goes no further, unless a structure is being placed or the command card is in build mode: then it reaches the controls first, which cancel the placement, or with none armed turn build mode off (owner, 2026-10-27; [ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 2). While the designer's name takes typing, the designer has Esc first ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 8). With no window open, Esc reaches the controls, which cancel what they have armed; in a universe, with nothing armed (`PlayerControls::IsArmed`) and build mode off, it opens the leave window ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 11).
6. **A window opens from a button on its structure's selection panel, and from its key in a match** ([ADR-068](ADR-068-interface-polish.md) decision 4 holds the keys): D for the designer, P for production and R for research, each of which also closes its window. F1 opens and closes the Controls window, which no structure has (ADR-068). O opens and closes the orders window, and the away window opens itself when the report of a time away arrives; neither belongs to a structure ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md)). Esc opens the leave window in a universe, and no key toggles it ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 11). What the Constructors build is no window: it is the command card's build mode ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 2). A window follows the selection: selecting a Shipyard alone while the designer is open makes it the designer's target, and selecting a producer alone while the production window is open shows it there.

## Consequences

- **Esc cancels a placement, and then turns build mode off, before it closes a window, and closes a window before it cancels anything else the controls have armed.** A placement armed from the command card is canceled by the first Esc, and build mode stays on until the next. With a window open and an attack-move or a standing order armed, a second Esc reaches the controls, and in a universe, once nothing is armed, the next opens the leave window.
- **The title bar is hatched by the pixel shader**, so a window costs a handful of quads beyond its content.
- **`GameAppTests` checks it without a GPU:** the manager's order, drag and settling and its slots (`WindowManagerTests`, and the engine's stack in `WindowStackTests`, ADR-100), and the designer's window, its layers, a HUD button under it, and its clamping on two screens (`HudTests`). Esc's order is `GameClient`'s and the controls', which need D3D12, DirectXMath and Windows; `PlayerControlsTests` checks the controls' part, and CI builds and runs both.

## What this forecloses

- Docking, resizing and minimizing windows: a window has the size its content gives it.
