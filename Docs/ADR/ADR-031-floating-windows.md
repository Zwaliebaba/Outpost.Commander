# ADR-031 — Windows float over the HUD in layers, their state in a manager and their geometry in the layout

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-08
Owns: floating-windows, window-state, window-placement, window-layers, window-opening

## Context

The game's screens float as windows rather than sit in one place: the designer, the research and a structure's production queue float, and the Ore, the minimap, the selection panel and the buttons stay anchored. The structures the Constructors build float in a window of their own, which the player opens when it wants it and moves out of the way of what it is doing, rather than stand in the buttons' panel at the bottom of the screen (owner, 2026-10-27). A window is dragged by its title bar and kept on the screen, comes to the front when clicked, closes with its × or with Esc, and keeps its place; the match runs on while one is open (owner, 2026-10-02). A place the player leaves a window in is kept across sessions too (owner, 2026-10-27), which [ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md) owns. The HUD is laid out afresh every frame by `Hud::Lay`, which keeps no state and draws nothing (ADR-015): it lists panels, lines, sprites, texts and buttons in pixels. Input focus is a rectangle test on that list.

## Decision

1. **`Outpost::WindowManager` holds the state, and nothing else.** Which windows are open and in what order front to back, where each was left, which is being dragged and where it was taken hold of, all in the HUD's reference units (ADR-006), and which of the two places under the Ore an unmoved production or research window took as it opened. It knows no geometry, so it is tested on its own. `GameClient` owns one for as long as the game runs; leaving a match closes every window but keeps their places. The places the player leaves are kept from one session to the next by [ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md), which puts them back with `WindowManager::Place`.
2. **`Hud::Lay` lays each open window out, back to front, after everything else.** It takes the manager, and places each window where the manager left it, or at the window's first place: the designer's top-right corner; for production and research the first of the two places under the column's kept place ([ADR-066](ADR-066-production-status-on-the-hud.md) decision 3) that no open window held when it opened; the orders window at the right, at the same height; the Controls, away and leave windows in the middle of the screen; and the build window at the bottom right, beside the selection and its buttons where they leave it room at their widest, and otherwise at the right edge with its foot a gap above the minimap's top, so that it does not move as the selection changes. A window is moved only as far as `Hud::KeepOnScreen` needs to keep its whole title bar, and 120 units of its width, on the screen. The clamp is applied when the window is laid out, so a smaller screen moves it back too, and `GameClient` settles the manager on the clamped place, so that a window dragged past an edge moves again as soon as the pointer comes back. Without a manager, `Lay` shows every window the content has at its first place, which is what the HUD's tests rely on.
3. **A window is a frame, a hatched title bar, a close box and four corner brackets** (ADR-030), opened by `OpenWindow`, which records where in the layout's lists of panels, lines, texts, sprites and buttons the window starts.
4. **The layout is drawn and clicked in layers.** Layer 0 is the HUD and layer i + 1 is the i-th window back to front; each layer's panels, then its lines, then its sprites, then its texts, so that a window covers the text of the window behind it. The bars over the entities come before every layer ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 5). `Layout::LayerAt` finds the front layer under a point, and `ActionAt` looks for a button only in that layer, so a HUD button under a window takes no click. A press on any window is the HUD's, not the world's ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 5).
5. **Input.** A press on a window brings it to the front; on its close box it closes it, and on its title bar it starts a drag that follows the pointer until the button is released. Esc closes the front window and goes no further, unless a structure is being placed: then it reaches the controls first, which cancel the placement (owner, 2026-10-27). While the designer's name takes typing, the designer has Esc first ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 8). With no window open, Esc reaches the controls, which cancel what they have armed; in a universe, with nothing armed (`PlayerControls::IsArmed`), it opens the leave window ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 11).
6. **A window opens from a button on its structure's selection panel, and from its key in a match** ([ADR-068](ADR-068-interface-polish.md) decision 4 holds the keys): D for the designer, P for production and R for research, each of which also closes its window. F1 opens and closes the Controls window, which no structure has (ADR-068). O opens and closes the orders window, and the away window opens itself when the report of a time away arrives; neither belongs to a structure ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md)). Esc opens the leave window in a universe, and no key toggles it ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 11). B opens and closes the build window, and so does the Build button that stands in the buttons' panel for a selection holding a Constructor ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 2); it never opens by itself (owner, 2026-10-27). It holds a button for each structure the Constructors build (`Hud::DescribeBuild`), with its cost or its reason in its cost's place ([ADR-043](ADR-043-hud-in-the-windows-look.md) decision 4); with no Constructor selected, every one is dim and says NO CONSTRUCTOR, so that the window keeps its size. Picking a structure arms its placement and leaves the window open. A window follows the selection: selecting a Shipyard alone while the designer is open makes it the designer's target, and selecting a producer alone while the production window is open shows it there.

## Consequences

- **Esc cancels a placement before it closes a window, and closes a window before it cancels anything else the controls have armed.** A placement armed from the build window is canceled by the first Esc, and the window stays. With a window open and an attack-move or a standing order armed, a second Esc reaches the controls, and in a universe, once nothing is armed, the next opens the leave window.
- **The title bar is hatched by the pixel shader**, so a window costs a handful of quads beyond its content.
- **`GameAppTests` checks it without a GPU:** the manager's order, drag and settling (`WindowManagerTests`), and the designer's window, its layers, a HUD button under it, and its clamping on two screens, and the build window's first place at 100% and 125% (`HudTests`). Esc's order is `GameClient`'s, which needs D3D12 and Windows, so it has no test; CI builds it.

## What this forecloses

- Docking, resizing and minimizing windows: a window has the size its content gives it.
