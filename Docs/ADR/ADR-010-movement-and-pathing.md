# ADR-010 — Ships path on a visibility graph, steer by heading, and part without colliding

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-07
Owns: path-graph, group-formation, waypoint-following, ship-separation, movement-data

## Context

Every obstacle is a circle on a plane (Game §map.plane). Ships path round the obstacles and, moved as a group, hold a loose formation at the pace of the slowest ship; they avoid overlapping but do not collide physically (Game §orders.movement). All of it runs in the tick, which has a budget ([`Docs/Budgets.md`](../Budgets.md)). [ADR-009](ADR-009-deterministic-core.md) makes the tick the only clock and allows floats, since a replay only has to reproduce on the same binary.

## Decision

1. **Paths come from a visibility graph over grown circles.** `Outpost::Pathfinder` grows every obstacle by the ship's footprint radius plus a 0.5 m margin and rings each grown circle with a polygon whose edges touch it: 16 corners, or 8 for a grown circle no more than 60 m in radius, such as a structure's, which keeps the polygon within 5 m of it. Two corners are joined when the line between them touches both polygons at those corners and nothing blocks it. A shortest path round convex obstacles bends only at corners and leaves each one along such a line, since a line that cuts into a polygon could be shortened, so the other pairs are never tested. Whether a line touches a polygon is a few multiplications; which obstacles a line is tested against is [ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md) decisions 1 to 3's. A path is the shortest route through that graph from the ship to its goal, found by A* with the straight-line distance. When the straight line is clear, the path is the goal alone. On a map of circles this gives shortest paths without a grid. The search checks lazily whether the start sees a corner: each corner is first given the straight line from the start, and the line is tested only when the corner comes off the queue.
2. **One graph per footprint radius, kept as the structures change.** The graph depends only on the obstacles and the radius. `InProcessServer` has one built for each hull's radius and the Constructor's, so that an order pays only for its search: when the graphs are built and brought up to date is [ADR-032](ADR-032-order-ticks.md) decisions 1 and 2's, and what placing or destroying a structure does to them is [ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md) decisions 5 to 7's. Structures block movement ([ADR-016](ADR-016-base-building.md)), and a ship whose remaining path a new structure blocks searches again at once. The graphs are a cache: `Simulation`'s equality ignores them.
3. **A goal inside an obstacle moves to its edge.** A ship ordered onto an asteroid stops beside it, and a goal beyond the map's edge is pulled inside it.
4. **A group moves as a formation, along one route.** A move order for several ships lays a grid of slots around the destination, facing the way the group travels, with three footprint radii of the group's widest ship between neighbors. The ships that are ahead now take the front slots, so few paths cross. The group searches once, from its ships' mean position to the destination at its widest ship's clearance (`Outpost::GroupRoutes`; owner, 2026-09-30). Each ship goes straight to its slot when it can. Otherwise it joins that route at the furthest corner it can see and follows it, through the destination when the route's end does not see its slot. On a Move or attack-move order it follows a lane of its own beside the route's corners ([ADR-047](ADR-047-a-fight-seen-whole.md) decision 1). Only a ship that sees no route, or whose way along one would be more than 1.5 times the straight line to its slot, searches for itself. Its path then becomes a route the ships after it may join, so ships cut off from the group together pay for one search. The limit matters because every ship cruises at its path's length divided by the time the slowest ship needs for its own path, so all arrive together and none goes faster than it can: one ship's detour would slow the whole group. An order for more than 32 ships plans its paths over two ticks, and its ships set off together in the second ([ADR-032](ADR-032-order-ticks.md) decision 3). A single ship is a group of one and searches for itself.
5. *(retired)*
6. **Corners are passed loosely and slots exactly.** A ship drops the next corner of its path once it is within its own radius of it, or can see the waypoint after it. A corner of its lane it drops at sight only from within three of its footprint radii ([ADR-047](ADR-047-a-fight-seen-whole.md) decision 2). Only the last waypoint, its slot, has to be reached. A ship that has tried to move for a second without getting 0.1 m closer to its waypoint gives it up: it passes a corner, or counts itself arrived at its slot. A ship coming about is not stalled ([ADR-039](ADR-039-ships-turn-in-arcs.md) decision 4). This is what ends every jam: ships crowding round the same corner, and slots that an obstacle or the edge pushed onto each other.
7. **Ships part, they do not collide.** After moving, each pair of overlapping ships is pushed apart along the line between their centers, in identifier order. Two moving ships, or two idle ones, part half each. A ship standing to fire keeps its range (owner, 2026-10-01): pushed, it gives way only sideways round its target, half the overlap, never toward it or away, and a moving ship that pushed it takes the rest. Pushed straight at its target, it steps to the side its identifier picks. An idle ship holds its ground against a moving one, which slides round it. A group closing on an enemy so spreads its front into an arc at range, and its rear ships find their own places on it. Then a ship inside an obstacle leaves it by the shortest way, and a ship past the edge is pulled back. There is no momentum and no damage. Checking every pair is O(n²), which is 20,000 pairs at 200 ships.
8. **Movement numbers are data.** Each hull has `footprintRadiusMeters` and `turnRateDegreesPerSecond`, and each drive a `turnRateFactor`, in `OutpostCommander/Assets/Tuning.json` ([ADR-008](ADR-008-tuning-data.md)). The footprints are the hulls' sizes, and the turn rates are final (owner, 2026-10-01). `MovementFor` combines them with the hull's speed and the drive's speed factor. The server refuses to start when a hull's footprint is wider across than the map's minimum gap ([ADR-008](ADR-008-tuning-data.md)), because such a ship could be walled off.

## Consequences

- **A very large group's formation outgrows the space.** Two hundred ships make a grid about a kilometer across. Slots past the edge or inside obstacles are moved clear, some land on each other, and the stall rule settles those ships near their slots. The formation is loose, as the design asks, and the ships do not overlap.
- **Arrival is a position, not a promise.** A ship that stalls "arrives" where it stopped, up to about a footprint from its slot.
- **A structure placed or destroyed costs each graph in use an extension or a reduction** ([ADR-054](ADR-054-obstacle-grid-and-extended-graphs.md) decisions 6 and 7).

## What this forecloses

- Grid or navigation-mesh pathing, flow fields and steering behaviors such as boids, without a new ADR.
- Physical collision between ships: ships part, but they do not bounce or damage each other.
- Formation shapes other than the grid, or formations that turn with the group on the way. The grid faces the direction of travel when the order is given.
