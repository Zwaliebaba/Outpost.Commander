# ADR-100 — The canvas: the interface's display list, layout, interaction and widgets are the engine's

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-27
Owns: display-list, canvas-layout, canvas-interaction, canvas-window-stack, canvas-theme, canvas-widgets, windows-on-the-canvas, windows-table

## Context

Every window of the interface is placed by hand: `Hud.cpp` sets each row a number of reference units from its window's corner, which `Painter` scales, and works each window's height out a second time apart from its layout. The pointer's hover reaches a window as a parameter, and the one field that takes keys reads its own. The owner asked for generic windows, buttons and drop-downs, so that a window is built and not drawn (owner, 2026-10-27), and decided that what is generic is the engine's, in `NeuronClient`, from the first task (owner, 2026-10-27). None of it needs a game concept, so R9 puts it there, and `NeuronClientTests` tests it without a GPU. R14 rules out a toolkit such as Dear ImGui, RmlUi or Noesis, and none draws the mockup's look without being rebuilt to it.

## Decision

1. **The display list is the engine's** (`Neuron::DrawList`). It holds panels (`CanvasRect`), solid, hatched or filled with an image (`CanvasFill`); lines at any angle (`CanvasLine`); sprites (`CanvasSprite`) and texts (`CanvasText`), which name a sprite and a font by their index in the atlas; regions (`CanvasRegion`), each with the widget it is, the caller's payload, whether it answers the pointer only, whether it scrolls, and whether it is a drop-down's field, which a press opens; and clips (`CanvasClip`), each a rectangle and its share of the panels, sprites and texts, which are drawn cut to it, as what a scroll view holds is (decision 6). It is divided into layers: the first, and one for each `BeginLayer`, which takes the rectangle a press on it falls in and whether it is a drop-down's overlay.
   - **Finding what is under a point:** `LayerAt` gives the front layer that holds a point. `HitAt` gives the region of that layer that takes a press, `PointedAt` the one the pointer is over, and `ScrollAt` the innermost one that scrolls. `Covers` says whether the point is on any panel ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 5). A region of a layer behind takes nothing.
   - **Drawing it:** `UiPipeline::Draw` draws it into the frame's one draw call ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 2), layer by layer, each layer's panels, then its lines, then its sprites, then its texts ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decision 4). A `FillStyle` says how wide a hatched panel's stripes are and which part of the image an image panel shows.
   - **Cutting to a clip:** an item a clip shares is cut to it on the CPU as it is drawn. `ClipQuad` cuts each of its quads to the rectangle, its texture coordinates with it, so that a glyph or a sprite cut short shows what it showed there, and the interface stays one draw call. A solid quad samples one texel at every corner, and a hatched one's stripes are laid on the screen's pixels ([ADR-030](ADR-030-typography-and-sprites.md) decision 6), so both keep their coordinates.
   - **Clipping a line:** `ClipToRect` cuts a line where it leaves a rectangle, as the minimap's view is cut. A clip never cuts a line; only the minimap draws them.
   - **What the game keeps:** `Hud::Layout` is a display list and what the game keeps beside it. That is the actions and previews that its regions' payloads index, the minimap's square and how it maps the map, its windows, and the bars over the entities. `Hud::FontOf` and `Hud::SpriteOf` give a typeface's and a sprite's index. `GameClient` fills the image from the fog's texture ([ADR-052](ADR-052-fog-texture.md)).
2. **A layout measures, then places** (`Neuron::LayoutTree`), recorded afresh each frame.
   - **Nodes:** a box, which is a spacer when it grows; a column, a row, a grid of a number of columns, or a stack, each over the others; a text; and a scroll, which holds a node across its width and as tall as it is, however tall the scroll is, standing it its offset above the scroll's top, the offset held so that what it holds still covers the scroll. The root is the screen.
   - **Style:** each node has a size given, or a size measured from what it holds and its padding, held between its least and its most. It also has a gap, an alignment across and down, a share of the room left along its parent's axis (`grow`), and an anchor to a corner, an edge's middle or the middle, inset by a margin. An anchored node takes no room in its parent. One larger than its parent's content box less its margin, across or down, stands at that box's start that way, so that its top-left corner, where a window's title bar is, stays in the box. A node may belong to a height group, whose nodes are all as tall as the tallest of them wherever they stand, and what holds each grows with it; one measured and not drawn takes part too, as a topic not on the page does (decision 7).
   - **Measuring and placing:** `Arrange` measures widths from the leaves up and gives them out from the root down. It then measures heights from the leaves up and places from the root down, so that a text wraps to the width it is given and its parent is as tall as its lines.
   - **Sharing room:** room left or lacked along a column or a row is shared in proportion among what grows. A node held at its least or its most keeps that, and what it could not take is shared again.
   - **Giving up room:** a node is measured at its least as well: a scroll holds nothing at its least, and a column's children that grow give up what they can, down to theirs. A column is never held shorter than that, by its most or by its parent, so that a window held to a most keeps what does not scroll, and its scroll view gives up the room.
   - **Text:** a text is set as one line, as one line cut short with three dots, or wrapped ([ADR-061](ADR-061-measured-text.md) decision 3). One cut short within a most is as wide as it is then set, so that what follows it stands after its dots. A text of nothing, or of which nothing fits, is still a line tall, so that what follows it keeps its place.
   - **The measure:** `Neuron::TextMetrics` measures, cuts and wraps a line as `UiPipeline::DrawText` sets it ([ADR-061](ADR-061-measured-text.md) decision 1), naming a font by its index. `Hud::TextMetrics` is the same measure named by the game's typefaces.
   - **Units:** sizes are reference units, and become pixels as they are drawn, times the scale. A tree measured at another scale, with fonts rasterized at it, stands where it stood in units ([ADR-070](ADR-070-interface-scale.md)).
3. **What every widget shares is one interaction state** (`Neuron::Interaction`).
   - **Identifiers:** a widget's identifier is a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of its label or its index under the identifier pushed last, such as its window's. A byte before them tells a label from an index. Each is claimed once a frame (`Claim`), and the canvas fails on a second claim ([ADR-093](ADR-093-a-failed-assertion-ends-the-process.md)).
   - **Hot, active and focused:** it keeps the widget under the pointer (hot), the one pressed (active) and the field that has the keyboard (focused). Input is resolved against the display list drawn last:
     - A move makes the widget under the pointer hot, but while one is active, only that one can be.
     - A press on a panel is the canvas's, and makes the widget under it active ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 5); a press on a drop-down's field opens it.
     - A release is never the canvas's, so it reaches the controls too. It clicks the active widget if it is let go over it.
     - The wheel is the canvas's only over a region that scrolls, whose offset takes it.
     - A widget dragged while it is active, such as a scroll view's thumb, takes how far down the pointer has moved since the press, or since it last took it (`TakeDragPixels`).
   - **Typing:** a field that has the keyboard takes every character and its keys in the order they came, Backspace, Enter and Esc ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 8). Enter and Esc give the keyboard back, and so does a press anywhere but the field, and a build that does not build the field, as when its window has closed. While a field has it, `TakesKeys` says that no order, group or camera key reads one.
   - **Esc:** Esc reaches the field that has the keyboard first, then an open drop-down, and only then goes on to the windows and the controls ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decision 5).
   - **A drop-down:** one is open at most. A press of any button outside its overlay closes it and goes no further, to the canvas, the windows or the controls (`Dismiss`), and so does Esc. A press on a choice picks it and closes it, and a build that does not build it closes it, as when its window has closed. The interaction keeps what the last build built (`WasBuilt`), by which a list that was not drawn last frame opens with its pick at its top.
   - **The store:** a scroll's offset and a caret are kept per identifier.
4. **The window stack is the engine's** (`Neuron::WindowStack`), over windows named by any value below a count, such as `WindowKind`. It keeps which windows are open and in what order front to back, where each was left and which is being dragged. A window opens at the front, is taken by its title bar, follows the pointer where it was taken hold of, and is settled where the layout could lay it out.
   - **Kept on the screen:** `Neuron::KeepOnScreen` keeps a window's title bar on the screen, and as much of its width as the caller keeps, or all of it when it is narrower.
   - **The game's part:** `Outpost::WindowManager` keeps the game's windows on a stack, and the places under the Ore that are the game's ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decision 1). `Hud::KeepOnScreen` keeps 120 units of a window's width.
5. **The look is a theme** (`Neuron::Theme`): the Style Guide's roles as one struct. It holds the colors of:
   - a window's body, its title bar, its close box and its corners;
   - a card, a pick and a field, and a disabled small button;
   - an edge, lit under the pointer and lit when picked;
   - the text, dim text, a label, a key, locked text and a figure;
   - the warning;
   - a bar's track and its fill;
   - a table cell's detail line.

   It also holds which font a window's title, its close box's mark, a name, a label, a figure and a table cell's detail line take, the title's color, where it and the mark stand and how far the title is tracked, the corner's and the tick's sprites, and the sizes and gaps the widgets are drawn with, in reference units. The game fills it with its own values (`GameTheme`): a window's title is its header in the label face at the body's inset, as each window drew its own.
6. **The widgets draw into the display list what the HUD's helpers draw for the same thing** (`Neuron::Canvas`). Each is a node of the layout and what it draws there. The build records them in the order they are drawn, the HUD's first and then each window back to front. `Finish` arranges the layout and draws what was recorded, and its overlays after it. Given a front canvas, built and not finished, it arranges and draws the front's after its own and before the overlays of either, so that what the front holds stands over the canvas's layers and under an open drop-down's list.
   - **The widgets:**
     - a window (`BeginWindow`), a layer of its own with its frame, hatched title bar, close box and its mark, title, and a bracket at each corner ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decision 3). It stands with its top-left corner at a place, or, until it is moved, at an anchor of the screen less a margin (decision 2), and its body stretches across it. It is no taller than a most the caller gives, while a scroll view in it can give up room (decision 2). Its title bar and its close box are regions that name nothing for the caller to do (`NO_PAYLOAD`), by which a press drags or closes it. They come after what the window holds, so that a widget on the title bar, such as the designer's arrows, takes a press before the bar;
     - a panel of the HUD (`BeginPanel`), a window's body and a bracket at each corner, with no title bar ([ADR-043](ADR-043-hud-in-the-windows-look.md) decision 1); or one that begins a layer of its own over every layer drawn before it (`BeginLayerPanel`), whose press falls in it, so that it stands and takes a press over a window;
     - a box the game composes, its face, a hatch over it if it has one, and its edge, under what it holds; and a card, a box that takes a press naming its payload while it has one, or only answers the pointer with it and takes no press (`CardPress::Previewed`), as a locked part's card does ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 7). Neither is lit under the pointer;
     - a fill and a press at a node (`Fill`, `Press`), for what the game composes of its own: a rectangle of any color but the warning's, and a region that takes a press naming the caller's payload and draws nothing, as a line of the HUD's status panel is;
     - a label, line by line;
     - an icon, a sprite of the atlas at its node, such as Ore's gem;
     - a button ([ADR-043](ADR-043-hud-in-the-windows-look.md) decision 4). It is a card with its label cut short only where it meets its figure, the mark before its figure, its reason in its figure's place while it is disabled, or where a figure would stand on a button without one, its key's cap and a bar of the work under way; stacked (`ButtonContent::stacked`), its figure or its reason stands under its label instead, both from its left, the two lines in its middle, as the command card's structures do ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 2). It is dim and takes no press while disabled, and its edge is lit under the pointer, or where the caller says it is pointed at (`ButtonContent::pointed`), unless it is picked;
     - a small button with its mark in its middle, and a stepper, `< SHIPYARD 05 >`, its subject between the arrows that step it ([ADR-068](ADR-068-interface-polish.md) decision 1);
     - a row of cells with the pick lit ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 3);
     - a checkbox, which no window draws yet;
     - a progress bar;
     - a chip, its words in the window's color on a tag of the warning's ([ADR-085](ADR-085-one-meaning-per-color.md) decision 1);
     - a key cap ([ADR-068](ADR-068-interface-polish.md) decision 4);
     - a drop-down (`DropDown`): a field with its value and a mark, "v", at its right, in the face of a small button's arrows. Its list, while it is open, stands in an overlay drawn once everything else on the canvas is (`Finish`), as wide as the field and a row as tall as the field for each choice, the pick lit and the row under the pointer edged: under the field, or over it where the screen ends under it, and where it is taller than the room either way, on the side with more, its rows cut to it and scrolled by the wheel and its thumb as a scroll view's are. A choice's press names the payload the caller gives it;
     - a scroll view (`BeginScroll`), which holds what is built until `EndScroll` as tall as that is, cut to the view (decision 1), and which the wheel over it and its thumb, dragged, scroll. The thumb and its track stand in a lane at the view's right, in the bar's colors, only while the view shows less than it holds. Its offset is the interaction's, kept by its identifier (decision 3). What lies wholly outside the view is not drawn, and a region of what it holds is cut to the view, so that only what shows takes a press;
     - a text field ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 8), which says whether typing ended with Enter or with Esc, and whose text is in the warning's color while the caller refuses it. Its text stands in its content box, so that its padding keeps room for what the caller sets over the field, such as a label before the text and a count after it;
     - a table (`Table`), first used by the overseer ([ADR-103](ADR-103-the-overseer-application.md) decision 7): its headings in a row as wide as its rows, each in the label face and tracked, a press on one naming its column's payload (`TableColumn`), and the column that sorts it (`TableSorted`) lit, with "^" or "v" after its words for which way; and under them its rows (`TableRow`), in a scroll view that takes the table's height, each a card that takes a press naming its payload, its edge lit under the pointer, the picked one in the picked card's face and edge, a mark at its left in a color of the caller's, such as a side's, and each cell a figure over a detail line (`TableCell`), each cut short to its column. A column is a width of its own or a share of what the others leave. The canvas keeps no sort: the caller sorts its rows and gives them in the order shown.
   - **Places:** a place is drawn as reference units times the scale from the screen's corner. Glyphs stay on whole pixels ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 3).
   - **Only a chip fills the warning's color** ([ADR-085](ADR-085-one-meaning-per-color.md) decision 1). `Canvas::Fill`, which the game draws its own composites with, takes any color but that one, and fails on it (`MayFill`).
7. **The whole interface is built on the canvas:** the HUD's panels, the menu, and every floating window, the leave, away, Controls, orders, production and research windows and the designer. All of it draws through the display list (decision 1).
   - **The HUD:** its panels are panels of one canvas, each anchored where it always stands ([ADR-043](ADR-043-hud-in-the-windows-look.md) decision 1), built before the windows in the order they are drawn, the column under the Ore, the tag, the selection, the command card, then the minimap, with what each holds at its places in it. The hint and the banner are built with them on a front canvas, each a panel of a layer of its own, which the front window finishes with itself (`FinishWindow`), or, with no window open, which is finished after the HUD, so that they stand over every window ([ADR-015](ADR-015-ui-drawing.md) decision 4). The game composes the status panel's lines, the bars and their edges of fills and presses. The minimap's frame is a panel whose leaf the game draws the map's marks, fog and view into once the canvas has placed it. A button of the HUD is lit while its action is the one under the pointer, with every other that takes it ([ADR-088](ADR-088-selection-and-commands.md) decision 1), and so is a status line, on the cards' color.
   - **The menu:** a panel of a canvas of its own in the middle of the screen, its title, subtitle and notice, and its buttons, which nothing lights.
   - **A canvas a window:** each window is built on a canvas of its own, in the game's theme, and drawn into `Hud::Layout`, back to front. A button's payload is its action's place among the layout's actions, and a card's that previews its preview's place among the previews. The layout keeps the window's frame, title bar, close box, corner and first action, so that `ActionAt`, `HoverAt`, `WindowAt` and the drag read every window alike.
   - **What they hold:** the orders window's rows are steppers, and its hour, minute, sectors and target a drop-down between the arrows, as wide as a stepper leaves its value room ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md) decision 9). Its sections stand in room for their most, so that it does not jump as the form changes. The Controls window's lines are a scroll view at least 440 units tall, so that the window stands 500 units tall at its least ([ADR-070](ADR-070-interface-scale.md) decision 3).
   - **How tall a window stands:** no taller than the screen lets it, a margin from the screen's foot below its top, or from its top above its foot where it is anchored at the bottom, and between its margins in the middle. A window whose scroll views have no more to give up stands taller.
   - **Production and research:** their header is a hatched band under the title bar with its subject, stepped through or not, and the Ore in its box; their cards, a production option's and a topic's, are composites of the game's, cards holding labels, icons and fills, two to a row; and their queue is a row for each job, a box. Every topic card is as tall as the most lines any listed topic needs, the topics not on the page measured in the cards' height group and not drawn ([ADR-061](ADR-061-measured-text.md) decision 4).
   - **The designer:** the mockup's places are its layout's padding, gaps and anchors. Its header runs under the title bar, with its Shipyard's arrows on the bar; under it stand the name, the saved designs, a row of part cards for each slot, the bars beside the damage cards, and the footer. Its part cards run past its body's inset, to 14 units from its edge ([ADR-062](ADR-062-type-scale-and-contrast.md) decision 4), and a locked one previews. Its part rows are a scroll view, its thumb in those 14 units, at least a line of cards tall, and nothing above or below them scrolls, so that a part hovered anywhere in the rows still previews in the bars. Its name is the canvas's text field, and `Hud::Layout::typedName` carries what was typed into it for `GameClient` to give the designer ([ADR-017](ADR-017-research-and-the-designer.md) decision 8).
   - **The pointer:** `GameClient` keeps one `Neuron::Interaction` and moves it each frame over the HUD drawn last, before it lays the next out, and gives it each press and release before the windows and the controls, and the wheel, which scrolls a scroll view under the pointer, 48 units a notch, before it zooms the camera. While a drop-down is open, every press and Esc reach it before anything else. A button of a window on the canvas is lit while it is the hot widget, and no other with it, and so is a cell, the designer's retreat among them; the HUD's own buttons are lit from the action under the pointer, as before, each whose action is that one.
   - **A button's reason:** a button whose label has a cost's place, after a `|`, shows its reason there while it is disabled, even with no cost, as Give does; one without shows none.
   - **The drop-down's overlay is the front layer:** the orders window, the one that holds drop-downs, brought to the front by the press that opens one, lays its overlay after itself and after the hint's and the banner's layers, and its list closes once another window stands in front of it. `WindowAt` finds no window over the overlay, the hint or the banner, and a window's own layer is the one it began (`FinishWindow`).
8. **Every window is a row of one table** (`Outpost::WINDOWS`, `WindowTable.h`, in `GameApp`): which window it is; its name in the interface's file ([ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md) decision 3); its title, on its title bar, which the designer has none of, its own standing in its body; the key that opens and closes it ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decision 6), which the away and leave windows have none of; where it first stands; whether `WindowsFit` counts it ([ADR-070](ADR-070-interface-scale.md) decision 3); and whether its place is kept from one session to the next ([ADR-099](ADR-099-interface-settings-kept.md) decision 1), which every window's is.
   - **Where a window first stands** (`FirstPlace`) is an anchor of the screen less a margin, so that a window not yet moved stands there on any screen and at any step, and never moves as the HUD's panels change:
     - the designer at the top-right corner;
     - production and research beside the column under the Ore, each in the first of the two places there that no open window held when it opened, which are the window manager's, the column's edge being one that its content never moves ([ADR-031](ADR-031-floating-windows.md) decisions 1 and 2; [ADR-066](ADR-066-production-status-on-the-hud.md) decision 3);
     - the orders window at the right edge, level with those;
     - the Controls, away and leave windows in the middle.
   - **Fitting:** `WindowsFit` measures each window its row counts holding its most and standing at its least, as it is built on the canvas where nothing else is, on a screen of no size, so that its scroll views give up all the room they can, and with no font, since every line it holds is one line however wide. On the menu, with no match, it measures the Controls window alone; in a match it counts the HUD's bottom row as well ([ADR-070](ADR-070-interface-scale.md) decision 3).

## Consequences

- **`NeuronClientTests` tests the canvas without a GPU:**
  - `DrawListTests`: the layers, the regions, a line's clip, and a glyph's, a sprite's, a solid and a hatched quad cut to a clip.
  - `TextMetricsTests`: the measure.
  - `LayoutTreeTests`: columns, rows, grids and stacks with padding and gaps; least and most; an anchor to each corner, edge and the middle, and a node too large for its box at the box's start; text wrapped and cut, and a text of nothing a line tall; a height group, with a node measured and not drawn; a parent measured from its children; the same tree at two scales; and a scroll that gives up its room to a column held at its most, its offset held within what it holds.
  - `InteractionTests`: hot and active across frames, a press held through a move off and back, the front layer taking a press, the wheel over and off a scroll, focus and Esc's order, focus given back and a drop-down closed once no longer built, a drag measured, a drop-down opened by a press on its field and closed by a press outside it, which goes no further, and an identifier claimed twice.
  - `WindowStackTests`: the order and a drag kept on the screen at each edge.
  - `CanvasTests`: each widget's display list in a theme of the tests' own, a window at an anchor, a window's body across its width, a widget on a title bar pressed before the bar, a box, a card, a card that previews and an icon, a button pointed at and a press at a node, a button's figure and reason stacked under its label, a field's text in its content box, a scroll view cut to its view, scrolled by the wheel and its thumb, a table's headings, its sort, its rows, its pick and its cells, and its rows scrolled under its headings, and a drop-down opened, picked from, closed by Esc and by a press outside it, its list over its field at the screen's foot, and a long list scrolled.
- **`HudTests` and `WindowManagerTests` hold the windows on the canvas as they held them before,** the steps the windows fit among them, and `HudTests.ScrollsTheDesignersPartRows` the designer's rows scrolled, and `PicksAnOrdersFieldFromItsDropDown` the orders window's hour picked from its list.
- **A widget is a function object a frame,** an allocation each, until `Finish` draws it.
- **The canvas keeps no widget between frames:** only the interaction's state and the window stack last.

## What this forecloses

- A retained widget tree, which would have to be kept in step with the simulation: the canvas is built afresh every frame.
- Dear ImGui, RmlUi, Noesis or any other toolkit, without a new ADR (R14).
