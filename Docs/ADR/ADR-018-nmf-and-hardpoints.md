# ADR-018 — Meshes are baked from glTF sources into NMF files, which carry hardpoints

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-25
Owns: mesh-sources, mesh-bake, nmf-reader, mesh-frame, hardpoint-tags

## Context

The game needs to know where things attach to a model: where a shot leaves a ship and where its exhaust shows (ADR-019). The owner edits the meshes and their hardpoints in Blender. Blender's glTF is right-handed, and the game is left-handed (ADR-012's `XMMatrixLookAtLH`), so a mesh carried over unchanged would show as the left–right mirror of what Blender shows, with its left and right hardpoints swapped. What reaches the GPU must also be checked: the pixel shader normalizes a zero normal into NaN, so a vertex without one cannot be drawn.

## Decision

1. **A model's source is a binary glTF, `Art/Models/<Set>/<Model>.glb`, or one a level ([ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md)), edited with Blender's own glTF import and export.** No Blender add-on is written, and the game never reads glTF.
2. **`Tools/BakeMeshes.py` bakes each source into `OutpostCommander/Assets/Models/<Set>/<Model>.nmf`.** It uses the Python standard library only (R14 binds what the executable is built from, not tools). It refuses what the game cannot draw rightly, naming the file: anything but triangles, a vertex without a position and a normal or with a number that is not finite, a skin, a morph target or an animation other than a spinning part's ([ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md)), an extension the file requires (Draco or quantized meshes), a sparse accessor, and data outside the file. It bakes in world space, so in Blender a hardpoint may or may not be parented to its model, and transforms need not be applied. A material's name is its faces' paint ([ADR-095](ADR-095-paints-team-trim-and-light.md) decision 1), and nothing else of it is read; texture coordinates, cameras and lights stay in the source.
3. **The change of frame keeps the model as Blender shows it.** glTF is right-handed with +y up, +z forward and −x to the right, and Blender's exporter turns its own z-up frame into that one. A source point (x, y, z) is (z, y, x) in the game, and each triangle's corners are reversed. That maps front to +x, up to +y and right to right, and keeps triangles clockwise seen from their front, Direct3D's default. So in Blender a model faces −y with +z up.
4. **A hardpoint is an empty named `hp_<tag>`.** Blender's own `.001` suffixes may follow, and they set the order. The tag is lowercase letters and digits, at most 31 characters. Its frame is the empty's: forward is its +z, the axis Blender's Single Arrow display draws, up is its +y, and its scale, which must be uniform, is its size. The engine stores the tag and never reads it (R9). `Outpost::HardpointKindOf` in `GameApp` gives the tags their meaning, and a model with a tag the game does not know fails to load, naming the file. The game knows `gun`, `exhaust` (ADR-019) and `beacon` (decision 9).
5. **The NMF is read strictly by `Neuron::ParseNmf` in `NeuronClient`** into a `MeshData`: positions, normals, how open each vertex is ([ADR-092](ADR-092-baked-occlusion.md)) and its paint ([ADR-095](ADR-095-paints-team-trim-and-light.md)), 32-bit indices, hardpoints and spinning parts. Its layout is [ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md) decision 3's. It has no sections and no tolerance for other versions. The assets and the executable ship in one MSIX package, so a file never meets a game older or newer than itself, and a change of layout is a new version and a bake of every source. The reader rejects a file cut short, bytes left over, no triangles, an index past the vertices, a number that is not finite, a malformed tag, a hardpoint whose directions are not unit vectors at right angles within 0.001, and a size that is not positive. The message names the file and the byte offset. 32-bit indices match `MeshData` and the index buffer. The bounds are measured at load.
6. *(retired)*
7. **The `.nmf` files are committed, and CI proves them current.** The baker also works out each vertex's occlusion ([ADR-092](ADR-092-baked-occlusion.md)). A Linux job runs `python Tools/BakeMeshes.py --check`, which fails when any `.nmf` is not byte for byte what its source bakes to, or has no source. It also runs `--self-test`, which bakes small sources it builds and checks that every refusal fires. CPython's floats are IEEE doubles with no fused operations, so a bake gives the same bytes on every machine. Committing the output of a tool sits close to AGENTS.md §2's rule against committing what a build step generates. The bake is not a build step. Making it one would make the C++ build depend on Python, and the check gives the guarantee the rule is after: nothing committed can drift from its source.
8. **The sources are the owner's fleet** (owner, 2026-10-02): nine models a set, the `Small`, `Medium` and `Large` hulls, the `Constructor`, and a `CommandStation`, `Shipyard`, `ResearchLab`, `MiningRig` and `DefensePlatform`, the Command Station, the Shipyard and the Research Lab with a source a level ([ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md) decision 1); and the asteroids' three rocks ([ADR-027](ADR-027-rock-crease-lines.md) decision 1). Their hardpoints are authored in the sources: a `gun` where each armed model fires from and an `exhaust` at each nozzle of the hulls and the Constructor. A model's guns are its muzzles: a shot leaves from the one nearest its target (`NearestMuzzle`, [ADR-064](ADR-064-structure-upgrades.md) decision 11), so the Defence Platform's six all fire its one gun. An unarmed model carries none, the Mining Rig included (owner, 2026-10-25).
9. **A `beacon` is a light that blinks in its side's line color,** as on a mast or an antenna, so that a building says whose it is the way a flag does (owner, 2026-10-11). Its size is its lamp's radius.
   - **It is a glow drawn with the exhaust's pipeline** ([ADR-019](ADR-019-glows-and-exhaust.md)): one point of light, 2.5 lamp radii across at the model's scale and 0.9 of the side's line color (`EdgeColor`, [ADR-040](ADR-040-lines-over-dark-faces.md) decision 1), where the model's world matrix draws its hardpoint, a Mining Rig's stance included ([ADR-044](ADR-044-rig-stands-on-its-legs.md)).
   - **It blinks:** lit for 0.2 s of every 1.6 s (`BEACON_ON_SECONDS`, `BEACON_PERIOD_SECONDS`) on the view's clock, as a part spins ([ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md) decision 4).
   - **Its phase follows its entity's identifier,** a share of the period that steps by the golden ratio's fraction from one identifier to the next (`IsBeaconLit`). An entity always blinks the same way, and entities whose identifiers are within three of each other never light together.
   - **A structure being built shows none, nor does a remembered structure or a wreck.** A ship's and a pirate's beacons blink in their sets' line colors.

## Consequences

- **Every model winds as Direct3D expects.** `GameAppTests` checks that at least 99% of each shipped model's triangles wind clockwise about their normals.
- **Editing is Blender's import, edit and export, then a bake.** A source exported without +Y Up turns the model on its side, and the bake cannot see that. A wrong front shows on screen, and is fixed in the source rather than in data.
- **`GameAppTests` checks that every model has the hardpoints its role needs** in both players' sets: a gun and an exhaust on each hull, an exhaust on the Constructor, and a gun on the two armed structures. It also checks that every shipped exhaust points out of the back of its model, within 45 degrees of straight back and away from its center. An exporter that writes an empty's facing on its node's +z rather than +y leaves the arrow pointing up, and the bake cannot see that either.
- **No shipped model has a beacon yet:** one blinks once the owner puts an `hp_beacon` empty on a model and bakes it. `HardpointsTests` checks the tag's kind, the blink's time lit in a period and that it repeats, identifiers within three of each other never lit together, and a glow where the model is drawn, none while dark, none while built and none on a model without a beacon.
- **Identifiers further apart can light together:** five apart, their phases are 0.09 of a period apart, under the 0.125 a beacon is lit. A beacon's light is too short and too small for that to read as a pattern.
- **The server never reads a mesh.** Hardpoints are presentation, and `GameLogic` cannot include `NeuronClient` (ADR-002). The reader needs no GPU, so it can move to `NeuronCore` on the day the server needs one, for docking say.

## What this forecloses

- Editing a baked `.nmf`. Sources flow one way, and there is no NMF importer for Blender.
- Skinned or morphing meshes, and hardpoints on moving parts, until the format has a version for them. What a moving part may do is [ADR-045](ADR-045-levels-and-spinning-parts.md)'s.
- A mesh drawn in the mirror image of its source.
