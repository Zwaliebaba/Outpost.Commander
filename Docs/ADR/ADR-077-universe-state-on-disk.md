# ADR-077 — A universe is saved whole every minute, logs every command, and comes back from its newest save and the log since

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-09
Owns: universe-save-format, universe-state-version, saved-state-fields, universe-folder, universe-recovery

## Context

A universe keeps running, and it must survive a restart of its server. A match's command log is kept in memory ([ADR-009](ADR-009-deterministic-core.md)), which a restart loses. A universe needs (owner, 2026-10-08):

- the whole of `Simulation`'s state saved every 60 s of ticks, without a stall on the server's thread;
- the command log written to the universe's folder as each tick applies it;
- on start, the newest save and the commands since it, so that a universe comes back to where it was;
- a save pinned to a state version, not a build, so that a fix that changes nothing the universe holds loads the universe it crashed in (AGENTS.md R18);
- a universe that pauses while its server is down.

This ADR decides the format, where the field lists live, how a version is kept honest, and the folder's layout.

## Decision

1. **A save is written and read as a message is,** by `GameProtocol`'s `ByteFormat.h` (`ByteWriter`, `ByteReader`, the `Record` concept), from the field lists and enumerations' last values in `WireFields.h` and beside them in decision 2 ([ADR-060](ADR-060-quic-in-process.md) decision 4).
2. **Every type the state holds lists its fields with a structured binding,** so a field added to it and not to its list does not compile. The protocol's types list theirs in `WireFields.h`; the server's public types (`Entity`, `ShipDesign`, `ShipMovement`, `Obstacle`, `PlaneVector`, `GroupRoutes::Route` and `Band`, `SectorPlacement`, `DerelictPlacement`, and the design's components and stats) in `UniverseState.cpp`; and `Simulation`'s private types (`PlayerState`, `Sector`, `PirateOutpost`, `ScheduledOrder`, `PlannedOrder` and its `Member`) as friends beside their fields in `Simulation.h`. `ByteWriter` finds each by argument-dependent lookup.
3. **`Simulation::SavedFields` names every member of `Simulation` in one structured binding,** and returns the ones a save holds. A member added to `Simulation` therefore does not compile until it is either saved or named as one that is not. A save holds the tick, the entities, the designs, the players, the target rule, the PRNG's state, the pathfinder's obstacles, the planned group orders, the standing groups' counter, the scheduled orders and their counter ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md)), the map's obstacles, size and sectors, the outposts, how the match stands, and a universe's rules and each base player's start ([ADR-084](ADR-084-universe-without-an-end.md)). It does not hold what follows from those or what is not state: the tick rate (the universe's identity holds it), the path graphs, the observer, the tuning data and what it gives a player with no research, each player's research effects, and the last tick's shots, destructions and events.
4. **Loading builds again what is not saved.** `Simulation::LoadState` runs on a simulation made with the save's seed and tick rate and given its tuning data. It sets the PRNG's state (`Neuron::Random::SetState`), gives a new pathfinder the saved obstacles, whose graphs are built whole when next needed, and works out each player's research effects. A graph built whole is the graph the running universe had kept up to date, to the bit (ADR-054 decision 7), so a loaded universe runs on as the saved one would have.
5. **A save is a header, the state, the seats' reports and a checksum.** The seats' reports are what each seat's player missed while away, which the server keeps beside the state ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md)). The header is the save's kind (`OCUNIV` and two zero bytes), `UNIVERSE_STATE_VERSION`, the hash of the save's layout (`SaveLayoutHash`, decision 6), the universe's identity (its seed, its tick rate, and `DataHash` of the tuning data's and the map's files) and the tick. A 64-bit FNV-1a of everything before it ends the save. A save is refused when its checksum does not match, it is not of the version this build reads or not laid out as this build lays it out, it is of another universe, or it does not read exactly to its end.
6. **`UNIVERSE_STATE_VERSION` is kept honest by its layout's hash.** `ByteLayout` describes how `ByteWriter` lays a type out, as text: each value's kind and width, in order, through every record, vector, optional and variant, but no value. `SaveLayout` describes what a save holds after its header, the state (`Simulation::StateLayout`) and the seats' reports, and `UniverseStateTests.TheLayoutIsTheVersions` holds its hash to the one recorded for the version. A save's header carries the hash too, and a save whose hash is not this build's is refused. A field added, taken away, reordered or given another type changes the hash, and the test fails until the new layout is recorded (AGENTS.md R18). Until the game is live, `UNIVERSE_STATE_VERSION` stays 1 and a new layout's hash replaces version 1's (owner, 2026-10-09), so a save of the old layout is refused by its header's hash rather than loaded; once live, a change raises the version and records its hash beside the old. An enumerator added to the end does not change the layout, and an old save still loads.
7. **A universe is a folder.** `ServerDesc::universe` names it. `InProcessServer::UseUniverse`, at the end of setup, makes the server a universe's:
   - every tick's commands are appended to the folder's log before the tick applies them, and handed to the system before the next (`std::ofstream::flush`);
   - every `UNIVERSE_SAVE_SECONDS` (60) of ticks the server encodes the state between two ticks, on its own thread, and hands the bytes to the folder;
   - a thread of the folder's own writes each save to `Universe-<tick>.save.partial` and renames it to `Universe-<tick>.save`, and keeps the newest `SAVES_KEPT` (3);
   - each save begins a new log, `Commands-<tick>.log`; the logs from the oldest kept save on are kept. Ticks are written out to twenty digits, so that names sort in tick order.
   - A log record is the tick, the length of the command's bytes and the bytes, which are the wire's `EncodeMessage`.
   - Once a universe is the server's, its in-memory `CommandLog` holds the commands since its last save; the folder has the rest.
8. **A universe comes back from its newest save that reads whole.** `UniverseFolder::Recover` reads the saves from the newest back, passes over one that is cut short or altered, and gathers the commands logged from its tick on, through every newer log. A record cut short at the end of the newest log, which only a killed process leaves, is passed over; one anywhere else is a broken log and refused. A folder whose saves all fail to read is refused, never started anew. The server's factory makes the server with the save's seed, skips the starting bases, and `UseUniverse` loads the save and applies the logged commands tick by tick, which brings the universe to the tick after the last command it logged. It then saves at once, so that its log begins anew there; a save of a tick the folder holds already is not written again.
9. **A universe pauses while its server is down.** The tick host does not catch up: a universe that comes back starts its ticks from where it left off, and does not run the hours it missed (ADR-009 decision 4).

## Consequences

- **What a killed process keeps.** Every save renamed into place, and the log of every tick that ran up to the last that applied a command. The ticks after that one that applied nothing are lost, at most those since the last save. Nothing is forced to the disk, so a lost machine or power keeps only what the system had written.
- **On another build that lays the same state version out alike,** a universe comes back from its save and applies its logged commands, and reaches a universe that follows the same orders. It is not promised to be the same to the bit, since floats replay only on the same binary (ADR-009 decision 1), and no player can tell: the server holds the only copy.
- **Every change to what `Simulation` holds carries a cost:** the field list and the recorded layout, and once the game is live the version; until then a change drops every save of the old layout. That is the price of loading a universe in a later build at all. Upgrade steps between versions, so that a universe survives a change of layout, are not built.
- **A late save's cost is measured by a test.** `UniverseFolderTests.MeasuresALateSave`, run with `OUTPOST_UNIVERSE_MEASURE`, reports, for seeds 1 to 3, the size of a save of a universe at minute 60 of two AIs on the repository's map and how long it takes to encode, averaged over 20 encodings.
- **Tests.** `UniverseStateTests`: a universe at minute 15 loads equal, saves to the same bytes and shows both players the same snapshot a tick on; universes saved at minutes 6, 12 and 18 and loaded, given the commands the running universe applied, equal it at minute 24; a save cut short, altered, of another universe, of other data, of another version, laid out otherwise or not a save at all is refused; and the layout's hash is the one recorded. With the PRNG's state not restored, or the path graphs built over the map's obstacles alone, those tests fail. `UniverseFolderTests`: a new universe is saved at once; a universe left after 5.5 minutes and opened again, with any seed, equals the universe that ran; the newest three saves and their logs are kept; a broken newest save falls back to the one before; a last record cut short is passed over and one in an older log is refused; and a folder of other data, or with no save that reads, is refused.

## What this forecloses

- **Pinning a universe to a build.** A universe is pinned to its state version and its layout; a build that lays the same version out alike loads it.
- **Saving the path graphs,** or anything else that follows from the state.
- **A universe that catches up the time its server was down,** without a new decision.
- **A change to what `Simulation` holds, or to the seats' reports, without recording its layout** (AGENTS.md R18).
