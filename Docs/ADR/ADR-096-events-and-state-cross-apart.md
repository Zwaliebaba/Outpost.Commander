# ADR-096 — A tick's events cross reliably, and its state on a stream of its own that a newer one overtakes

Status: **accepted** · 2026-10-26
Owns: connection-streams, newest-state, event-ticks

## Context

A seat's connection carried every snapshot whole and in order on one stream ([ADR-060](ADR-060-quic-in-process.md) decision 2). A QUIC stream delivers its bytes in order, so a packet lost from one snapshot held back every snapshot behind it until the packet was sent again, a round trip or more later; and a link slower than the snapshots queued them on the server, each later than the last. A snapshot is many datagrams, even with its catalogue apart (ADR-060 decision 9): the 20th tick of a match crosses in 6.5 KB, and of the measurement load in 21.8 KB.

Most of a snapshot is state, which the next tick's state replaces: where the entities are, what they hold, who holds each sector. A newer state is worth more than an older one that has not arrived. The rest happened once and is seen only in the snapshot of its tick: the shots fired, the entities destroyed, the player's events ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md) decision 1) and its report of a time away. Lost, an explosion is never shown and a Mining Rig's alert never raised.

## Decision

1. **A connection has the client's stream, and a stream for each tick's state** (owner, 2026-10-26).
   - **The client's stream** is bidirectional, and the client opens it; the listener lets a client open that one and no more. Commands go up it. Down it go the welcome (ADR-060 decision 5), the catalogue (ADR-060 decision 9) and, for each tick that has any, the tick's events, `EventsMessage`: its shots, its entities destroyed, the player's events of it and its report of a time away (`TakeEvents`). It delivers them reliably and in order, with ADR-060 decision 2's framing.
   - **A tick's state**, `StateMessage`, is the snapshot without its catalogue and its events. It crosses on a unidirectional stream that the server opens for it and ends with it, so the stream's end is the message's. The client lets the server open `QuicChannel::NEWEST_STREAMS`, 16, at once (`PeerUnidiStreamCount`).
   - **The client's stream goes first.** It has MsQuic's highest priority, 0xFFFF, so its messages go ahead of a state when both have bytes to send. The state streams keep MsQuic's default, 0x7FFF, and MsQuic sends streams of one priority first come, first served, which is its default scheme.
2. **A seat has two states on their way at most, and nothing sent is abandoned** (owner, 2026-10-26). While two of a seat's state streams are not yet acknowledged, the server sends no state for the tick (`QuicChannel::SendNewest`), and the next tick's goes if one has arrived by then. A link too slow for every state so carries as many whole as it can, each the newest when it was sent, with no backlog beyond two. Aborting the older states to send the newest instead starved such a link: MsQuic's congestion control holds a lossy link below what the measurement load's 21.8 KB states take, so a state took longer than its turn to cross and was abandoned with its bytes spent. Measured in CI's Debug|x64 run of `ImpairedLinkTests.HoldsASeatsBacklogToTwoStatesOnTheHotspot` on 2026-10-26, with the newest two kept and older ones aborted, the client had 437 states in a minute of 1,200 ticks, with gaps of up to 2.2 s. A state the server cannot open a stream for, the client having `QuicChannel::NEWEST_STREAMS`, 16, open, is not sent either.
3. **The client hands on a snapshot for each state that arrives whole and newer than the last** (`QuicTransport`). A state that arrives after a newer one is dropped (`QuicChannel::ReceiveNewest`). A state names how many catalogues and events messages the seat's connection had been sent when it was sent (`StateMessage::orderedSent`), and waits until the client has taken as many, giving way to a newer state while it waits. It is then put together with the catalogue it was built against and the events of every tick up to its own not yet handed on, oldest first (`PutEvents`). So a snapshot handed on carries its own tick's events, and those of any tick whose state a newer one overtook.
4. **Each shot and destruction says its tick** (`ShotView::tick`, `DestroyedView::tick`), which `Simulation::Tick` gives it as the tick ends. The client plays each from one tick before its own, where the view shows it ([ADR-013](ADR-013-client-view-and-controls.md) decision 3), whichever snapshot brought it, and the match log counts a shot once, by its tick, shooter and target, at its own tick ([ADR-038](ADR-038-phase-one-match-log.md) decision 1). An event and a report of a time away carry no tick. The client reads them at the snapshot that brings them, late by the states overtaken.
5. **A seat whose connection has gone is sent nothing,** rather than having its states and events queued. A seat taken again ([ADR-078](ADR-078-dedicated-server.md) decision 4) is sent its catalogue first (ADR-060 decision 9), and its states and events from then on, the report of its time away among them ([ADR-080](ADR-080-events-and-scheduled-orders.md) decision 7).
6. **The engine knows no state or event (R9).** `QuicChannel::SendNewest` and `ReceiveNewest` move messages of which only the newest matters, and the game says which those are. `SendNewest` is called on the server's thread, never MsQuic's. A state stream's callbacks run on MsQuic's threads and touch the channel's state under its lock, and a stream is closed by its own last callback or by `Close`, whichever takes it out of the channel's list ([ADR-025](ADR-025-server-thread.md) decision 4, ADR-060 decision 7).

## Consequences

- **A state held up on the way holds up no other.** Each crosses on a stream of its own, and the view moves across a gap (ADR-013 decision 3). Events are never lost, only late where their state did not come.
- **`Snapshot` is unchanged but for the ticks on shots and destructions** ([ADR-002](ADR-002-authoritative-server.md) decision 4). The loopback, the hosted players, the AI's matches and the universe log read what they read, and every snapshot arrives there with its events. Shots and destructions are not in a save, so the save's layout is unchanged (AGENTS.md R18).
- **The wire's layout changed,** so a client of an older build is refused as `WrongVersion` (ADR-060 decision 5).
- **Tests.** `QuicChannelTests.CarriesTheNewestMessagesApart` sends two long newest messages and a third while both are on their way, and checks that the third is dropped, that each one handed on is newer than the last, and that the next goes once both have arrived. `WireFormatTests.CarriesATicksEventsApartFromItsState` takes a snapshot's events apart and puts them back to the snapshot built. `QuicTransportTests.HandsOnTheSnapshotsTheServerBuilt` checks two minutes of snapshots, tick by tick, against the ones the server built, events and all. `ImpairedLinkTests.CarriesEverySnapshotOfAMatchWhenClear` checks that through the relay with no impairment every tick's state arrives. `HoldsASeatsBacklogToTwoStatesOnTheHotspot` checks that over a minute of the measurement load on the hotspot profile a seat's backlog stays at two states and a second's events, and that states keep coming. `CarriesEveryTicksEventsOverWiFi` checks that through the Wi-Fi profile every tick's events of a fight arrive once and in order. `CombatEffectsTests.PlaysAShotFromItsOwnTickWhicheverSnapshotBringsIt` and `MatchLogTests.CountsAShotAtItsOwnTickWhicheverSnapshotBringsIt` check the client's part. The QUIC tests run in CI's Debug|x64 run only.

## What this forecloses

- **QUIC datagrams for state.** A state is many datagrams, and cutting it up and putting it back together is reliability written by hand under another name. Changing that changes this ADR.
- **A client that holds a newer state back for an older one.** A reader that needs every tick's state reads the server in-process, through the loopback.
